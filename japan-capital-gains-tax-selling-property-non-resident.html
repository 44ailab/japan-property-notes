Capital Gains Tax in Japan When a Non-Resident Sells Property

Last reviewed: November 21, 2026

Quick answer

A non-resident selling Japanese property faces different national capital gains tax rates depending on how long the property was held as measured on January 1 of the sale year. The verified rates are 30.63% for five years or less and 15.315% for more than five years. Sale withholding is a separate calculation, and any amount withheld is credited on the seller’s final return.

Check the holding period first

The date I would check first is January 1 of the sale year. The holding-period test uses that date, so the number of years held on the actual day of sale is not the only date to consider.

For a non-resident, the national rate including the reconstruction surtax is 30.63% when the holding period measured on January 1 is five years or less. It is 15.315% when the period measured then is more than five years.

Non-residents do not pay inhabitant tax. From 2027, the components of the surtax change, but the combined rates stated here remain unchanged.

Separate the gain from the sale price

The capital gains rates above concern the taxable gain. Sale withholding, where required, is calculated on the gross sale price.

That distinction matters when looking at paperwork. A withholding amount is not the same calculation as the final capital gains tax. The seller credits the withholding on the final return, and an excess is refunded.

For the buyer’s withholding obligation, payment deadline and exception, see the related page: Japan Property Sale Withholding Tax for a Non-Resident Seller.

Check the residential reduced rate carefully

There is a 10.21% reduced rate on the first ¥60 million of taxable gain for a qualifying residential sale. The stated conditions include use as the seller’s own residence for 10 years or more and sale within three years of moving out.

This reduced rate does not apply to investment property. I would keep that boundary clear: owning a property for a long time does not, on its own, establish that this residential treatment applies.

If you think a former home may qualify, ask a Japanese tax professional to check the facts before using the reduced rate in a calculation.

Reconcile withholding on the final return

Where sale withholding applies, the buyer generally withholds 10.21% of the gross price. An exception applies when the buyer is an individual buying the property for their own or a relative’s residence and the price is no more than ¥100 million.

The seller then credits any withholding on the final return. Excess withholding is refunded. I would keep the sale documents and withholding information together so the tax professional preparing the return can reconcile them.

The residential exception to withholding and the residential reduced capital gains rate are different rules. One looks at the buyer and price; the other concerns the seller’s qualifying use and holding period.

FAQ

Is the five-year test measured on the sale date?

The stated holding-period test is measured on January 1 of the sale year.

Can an investment property use the 10.21% residential reduced rate?

No. The residential reduced rate does not apply to investment property.

Is the amount withheld necessarily the final tax?

No. Withholding is credited on the seller’s final return, and an excess is refunded.
