Inheritance Tax in Japan for Foreign Heirs

Last reviewed: November 21, 2026

Quick answer

Japanese inheritance tax can apply to property located in Japan regardless of the nationality or residence of the deceased person or heir. The basic deduction is ¥30 million plus ¥6 million multiplied by the number of statutory heirs. A required return and payment are due within 10 months from the day after learning of the death.

Start with the Japanese property

When I check this question, I begin with the location of the property. A foreign heir living outside Japan should not assume that Japanese property is outside Japanese inheritance tax simply because the heir has no Japanese passport or does not live in Japan.

The verified rule here concerns property located in Japan. It does not establish the treatment of every other asset in an estate. I would ask a Japanese tax professional to assess the estate rather than extending this property rule to assets whose treatment has not been checked.

Inheritance tax and inheritance registration are also different topics. This page concerns the tax side.

Calculate the basic deduction

The basic deduction is:

¥30,000,000 + ¥6,000,000 × the number of statutory heirs

For example, with one statutory heir, that formula gives a basic deduction of ¥36 million. With two statutory heirs, it gives ¥42 million.

The number in the formula is the number of statutory heirs. I would confirm that number before relying on an estimate. The formula is a starting point for checking the tax position, not a complete calculation of an individual heir’s tax.

Understand the rates and possible surcharge

The inheritance tax schedule has eight brackets, with rates from 10% to 55% applied to each heir’s notional statutory share. The schedule also uses quick-calculation deductions.

A 20% surcharge applies to heirs other than a spouse or first-degree blood relative, with representation heirs excepted. The heir’s relationship to the deceased therefore matters as well as the property and estate figures.

I would not select a rate by looking only at the value of a single property. The verified calculation framework uses notional statutory shares, and the relationship-based surcharge is a separate question.

Put the filing deadline on the calendar

A required inheritance tax return and payment are due within 10 months from the day after learning of the death. Non-resident heirs file through a tax agent.

A non-resident who must file or pay in Japan appoints a Japan-resident tax agent and notifies the competent tax office. I would make that appointment part of the early checklist, alongside confirming the date on which the heir learned of the death.

If the heir later sells an inherited vacant house, a separate sale deduction may be relevant. See the related page: Selling an Inherited Vacant House in Japan: The Special Deduction.

FAQ

Does a foreign heir automatically avoid Japanese inheritance tax?

No. Japanese property can be subject to Japanese inheritance tax regardless of the heir’s nationality or residence.

Is the basic deduction the same for every estate?

No. The formula includes ¥6 million multiplied by the number of statutory heirs.

Does every heir pay the 20% surcharge?

No. The stated surcharge excludes a spouse and first-degree blood relatives, and representation heirs are excepted.
