Japan Property Purchase Stamp Duty on ¥10 Million

For a qualifying real estate sale contract stating ¥10,000,000, the reduced stamp duty is ¥5,000.
That reduced rate applies to contracts created by March 31, 2027.
The next band starts above ¥10,000,000; the exact boundary matters.

The boundary was the part I wanted to check. A table that says “¥5 million to ¥10 million” can leave a reader wondering what happens at exactly ¥10 million. The National Tax Agency’s reduced-rate table makes the distinction: the ¥5,000 band is over ¥5,000,000 through ¥10,000,000.

For a qualifying contract created by the stated deadline, exactly ¥10,000,000 falls in that band. A contract amount above ¥10,000,000 and up to ¥50,000,000 falls in the next stated band, with ¥10,000 in reduced stamp duty.

Read the contract amount and the date

The fact-checked reduced rates for real estate transfer contracts are:

Contract amountReduced stamp duty
Over ¥1,000,000 to ¥5,000,000¥1,000
Over ¥5,000,000 to ¥10,000,000¥5,000
Over ¥10,000,000 to ¥50,000,000¥10,000
Over ¥50,000,000 to ¥100,000,000¥30,000
Over ¥100,000,000 to ¥500,000,000¥60,000

These reduced rates apply to qualifying contracts created by March 31, 2027. If the contract falls outside that period, I would check the current NTA guidance and ask the professional handling the transaction which rate applies.

Do not turn the table into a total purchase-cost estimate

Stamp duty on a sale contract is one item. It does not include a brokerage fee, registration tax, a judicial scrivener’s fee, or real estate acquisition tax.

I would keep those costs separate. Registration tax, for example, uses the fixed-asset-tax assessed value rather than simply the sale price. Acquisition tax is a prefectural tax for which the notice arrives after purchase. Neither should be calculated by taking the stamp-duty figure and applying an assumed multiplier.

A document’s treatment also depends on the relevant facts. I would ask the licensed brokerage or a qualified professional which contract document is being considered and how the stamp-duty amount was determined. The table is a way to check the band; it is not a substitute for reviewing the actual document.

Questions I would ask before signing

  • What amount is stated in the sale contract for the stamp-duty calculation?
  • Is the contract within the reduced-rate period?
  • Which band is being applied?
  • How is the stamp duty handled in the transaction, and which party is responsible for the relevant cost?
  • Is the estimate for stamp duty shown separately from other purchase costs?

For the ¥10,000,000 example, the answer in the fact-checked reduced table is ¥5,000. I would still retain the NTA source with my budget and confirm the document and date when the transaction is prepared. That small check prevents a boundary mistake from carrying into the rest of the cost estimate.
