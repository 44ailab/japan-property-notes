Buying Investment Property in Japan: Questions I Researched

I put this page together to make the questions I researched easier to navigate. The answers are starting points; the individual pages go into more detail.

Eligibility

Can a foreigner buy investment property in Japan?

In the sources I reviewed, I found no general requirement to be a Japanese citizen to buy property in Japan. Buying and owning property, however, does not itself give you permission to live in Japan.

Read the individual page: can-foreigners-buy-investment-property-japan.html

Do I need permanent residency to buy property?

I found no general requirement to have permanent residency before buying property in Japan. Your residency can still affect practical matters, such as banking and reporting, so I would check those separately.

Read the individual page: buy-property-japan-without-permanent-residency.html

Does buying property give me a visa?

I found no Japanese residence status granted simply because someone owns property. You would need to qualify for a status of residence separately if you want to live in Japan.

Read the individual page: does-buying-property-japan-give-you-visa.html

Do I need to declare my nationality when registering ownership?

The Ministry of Justice guidance I checked says that individual owners must declare their nationality as part of the required search information when registering ownership. The information is held in an internal file and does not appear on the public registry certificate.

Read the individual page: japan-property-nationality-declaration-registration.html

Costs

What costs should I look at beyond the purchase price?

When I looked at used homes, I found that SUUMO suggests budgeting about 5–10% of the price for closing costs, including the brokerage fee. The actual amount depends on the property and transaction, so I would ask for an itemized estimate.

Read the individual page: japan-property-purchase-costs-10-million-yen.html

How is the brokerage fee capped?

For a property priced above ¥4,000,000, the legal cap on the brokerage fee is 3% of the price + ¥60,000 + consumption tax. For properties priced at ¥8,000,000 or less, a separate ceiling of ¥330,000 including tax applies.

Read the individual page: japan-real-estate-brokerage-fee-calculator.html

Is registration tax calculated from the purchase price?

The National Tax Agency table I checked uses the fixed-asset-tax assessed value as the basis, rather than the purchase price. The rate depends on what is being registered and whether a reduction applies.

Read the individual page: japan-property-registration-tax-foreign-buyers.html

When is real estate acquisition tax paid?

The guidance I reviewed describes this as a prefectural tax assessed after the purchase, rather than a bill paid at the closing table. The prefecture sends a payment notice, and timing varies by prefecture.

Read the individual page: japan-real-estate-acquisition-tax-non-residents.html

Is there a fixed legal fee for a judicial scrivener?

I could not find an official fee schedule: each office sets its own fees. I would ask for a quote that separates the scrivener’s fee from taxes and document costs.

Read the individual page: japan-property-purchase-judicial-scrivener-fees.html

Sending money

Do I need a Japanese bank account to buy?

The guidance I found suggests that a Japanese account is not necessarily required for a purchase. In general, opening an ordinary Japanese account can be difficult for someone who lives abroad and has no Japanese address, so I would confirm the payment route for the particular transaction.

Read the individual page: buy-property-japan-without-japanese-bank-account.html

Can I send the purchase money from overseas?

The sources I reviewed describe sending funds from abroad as a possible payment route. I would first ask the licensed brokerage and the receiving bank to confirm the recipient, payment instructions and timing for the transaction.

Read the individual page: transfer-money-overseas-buy-property-japan.html

What should I check before making an international transfer?

I would check the recipient’s details through a trusted channel, the currency, the amount the recipient must receive and the payment deadline. I would also ask the sending and receiving institutions about fees and any documents they need; I did not find one fixed fee or document list that applies to every transfer.

Read the individual page: transfer-money-overseas-buy-property-japan.html

Will I need to show where the money came from?

In the guidance I reviewed, bank statements and information about the source of funds came up as documents buyers may be asked to provide. There is no single checklist I can promise will apply, so I would ask the bank and brokerage what they need for the case.

Read the individual page: transfer-money-overseas-buy-property-japan.html

After purchase and when selling

Does a non-resident need to report a property purchase?

The Ministry of Finance guidance I checked says that a non-resident acquiring Japanese real property must report the acquisition through the Bank of Japan within 20 days. Whether someone is a non-resident for this purpose depends on residence, not simply nationality.

Read the individual page: japan-fefta-real-estate-report-non-resident.html

What annual property taxes should I expect?

Fixed asset tax is charged at the standard rate of 1.4% of the government’s assessed value. City planning tax may also apply in designated areas, with the rate set by the municipality; I would check the property’s actual tax information before relying on an estimate.

Read the individual page: japan-fixed-asset-tax-foreign-property-owner.html

Is rent paid to a non-resident owner subject to withholding?

The tax guidance I reviewed says withholding can apply, but it makes an exception when an individual rents the property as a home for themselves or their family. The owner’s filing obligations are a separate question, so I would discuss the actual tenancy with a Japanese tax professional.

Read the individual page: japan-rental-income-withholding-tax-non-resident.html

What happens to withholding tax when a non-resident sells?

The guidance I checked says the buyer may have to withhold part of the sale price when paying a non-resident seller. There is an exception for certain purchases by an individual for their own or their family’s home, and the withholding is a prepayment rather than necessarily the seller’s final tax.

Read the individual page: japan-property-sale-withholding-tax-non-resident.html
