The 1981 Seismic Standard in Japan: Check the Building Confirmation Date

To identify which seismic standard applies to a building in Japan, check when its building confirmation was issued. Japan’s new seismic standard took effect on June 1, 1981, but a completion date after that change does not by itself establish which standard applies.

Why the confirmation date matters

The key date is the building confirmation date, not the completion or registration date. A building completed in 1982 can still have been built to the pre-1981 standard if its confirmation was issued before June 1, 1981.

That distinction changes the first question I would ask when reading a listing. Instead of stopping at the stated completion date, I would ask for the building confirmation date and the record that establishes it. If that information is not immediately available, I would treat the applicable standard as something still to be checked.

A date can help identify the applicable standard; it cannot, by itself, tell me whether a particular building is safe. I would keep those questions separate.

The further change for wooden buildings

For wooden buildings, a further tightening took effect on June 1, 2000. It addressed foundation specifications matched to ground conditions, metal hardware at structural joints, and balanced placement of load-bearing walls.

If I were considering a wooden building, I would ask how the building confirmation date relates to that change as well. I would also ask whether records exist that can help a qualified inspector understand the building.

I would not infer the condition of the foundation, joints or walls from a date in an advertisement. Those details need to be assessed in the context of the specific building.

Ask about evaluations and retrofit records

Alongside the confirmation date, I would ask whether any seismic evaluation or retrofit records exist. If they do, I would want to know what they cover and have a qualified inspector assess them in relation to the building.

If no records are available, I would not fill the gap with an assumption that the building is either safe or unsafe. I would ask what can be checked and discuss the property with a qualified inspector.

This matters whether the listing emphasizes the building’s age or says little about it. The information I want is the confirmation date, any available evaluation or retrofit records, and an expert assessment of the specific building.

Questions to ask before you buy

  • When was the building confirmation issued, and what record shows the date?
  • Which seismic standard applies based on that confirmation date?
  • Are there seismic evaluation or retrofit records for the building?
  • Can a qualified inspector review the available records and assess the building?

FAQ

Does a building completed in 1982 automatically meet the new seismic standard?

No. A building completed in 1982 can still have been built to the pre-1981 standard if its building confirmation was issued before June 1, 1981.

Is the registration date the date to check?

The date relevant to which standard applies is the building confirmation date, not the registration date. Ask for the confirmation date and the record that establishes it.

Does the applicable standard tell me a building is safe?

No single listing detail can determine a specific building’s safety. Ask about the confirmation date and available seismic evaluation or retrofit records, then consult a qualified inspector.
