Stamp Duty on a ¥100 Million Property Contract in Japan

Last reviewed: November 22, 2026

Sitting with a draft contract for a ¥100 million property, I caught myself doing the wrong calculation again: scaling the stamp duty as a percentage of the price, the way the brokerage fee works. Stamp duty does not scale. It is a table of bands, and at this price the band edges matter — a contract written at exactly ¥50 million sits in a lower band than one written just above it. When I checked the band against the National Tax Agency's figures, the answer for a ¥100 million contract was ¥30,000 under the current reduced rates, and it disappears entirely if the contract is made electronically. This page is that worked example.

Quick answer

A real estate sale contract of ¥100 million falls in the band over ¥50 million through ¥100 million. The standard stamp duty for that band is ¥60,000; under the reduced rates for contracts created through March 31, 2027, it is ¥30,000. A contract concluded electronically carries no stamp duty at all. The band, not the exact price, decides the amount — a ¥60 million contract and a ¥100 million contract carry the same duty.

Why the band matters more than the price

Two properties at different prices can carry identical stamp duty, and two properties ¥1 apart can carry different duty, because the table is bands with strict edges. The edge that matters at this level is ¥50 million: a contract at exactly ¥50 million sits in the band below this one — over ¥10 million through ¥50 million, ¥10,000 under the reduced rates — while a contract at ¥50,000,001 moves into the ¥30,000 band this page is about. At the top, ¥100 million is the last price inside this band; contracts above ¥100 million move into the next band up. So for any contract between ¥50 million and ¥100 million, the answer is the same ¥30,000, and the negotiation over a few million yen of price does not move it. The full standard and reduced table, for every band, is on this hub's stamp duty page, linked below.

The electronic-contract exemption

The duty attaches to the paper document. A sale contract concluded electronically — exchanged and signed as electronic data rather than as a stamped paper original — carries no stamp duty. I checked this carefully because it sounded like a loophole; it is simply how the tax is defined. Whether a given transaction uses paper or electronic contracts is a matter for the parties and the brokerage, and the rest of the contract's terms are unaffected — but on this band the difference between the paper duty and zero is ¥30,000, worth a question before the format is chosen.

Where this sits in a ¥100 million purchase

Stamp duty is the smallest exact number in the purchase, and I keep it on the checklist because it is exact, not because it is large. On the same ¥100 million property, the legal cap on the brokerage fee is ¥3,060,000 before consumption tax — more than one hundred times the reduced stamp duty. Registration and license tax uses the fixed-asset-tax assessed value rather than the purchase price (land transferred by sale is reduced to 1.5% for registrations made by March 31, 2029; a building transferred by sale is 2.0%), and real estate acquisition tax, also on assessed value at a reduced 3% for land and houses for acquisitions made by March 31, 2027, arrives by prefectural notice some months after the purchase.
