Can Foreigners Buy Investment Property in Japan?

Foreign nationals can buy and own property in Japan without a Japanese visa or permanent residency.
For a non-resident, a FEFTA acquisition report is generally due within 20 days.
From October 5, 2026, an individual becoming a registered owner must declare nationality and other required information.

When I began researching this question, I found that “Can I buy?” is only the first part. The answer is generally yes, but ownership, immigration status, registration and post-purchase reporting are separate matters.

Buying property does not give the owner a visa or a right to live in Japan. If you intend to stay in Japan, check the immigration requirements for your circumstances separately.

Foreign ownership and residence

Japan does not require a buyer to hold a Japanese visa or permanent residency simply to buy or own property. The fact-check relies on the residence-status framework and consistent buyer guidance, including Housing Japan’s explanation.

The ability to own does not guarantee that every practical service will be available. For example, an overseas buyer without a Japanese address generally cannot open an ordinary Japanese bank account. A purchase does not require the buyer to hold one, and the payment route should follow the contract.

A non-resident’s FEFTA report

The Ministry of Finance guidance says a non-resident acquiring Japanese real property must report within 20 days of acquisition using Form 22. The report is in Japanese; a Japan-resident agent, such as a brokerage, may submit it.

For ownership acquisitions on or after April 1, 2026, the fact-check states that the former exemptions were removed regardless of purpose or amount. Residence determines non-resident status, not nationality alone. A Japanese citizen living abroad can be a non-resident; a foreign national living and working in Japan is generally a resident.

Failure to report or a false report can carry a fine of up to ¥500,000, or up to six months’ imprisonment. I would confirm who will prepare and submit the report and retain evidence of submission.

The nationality declaration

From October 5, 2026, an individual becoming a registered owner must declare nationality and other required search information with supporting proof. It applies to Japanese and foreign buyers, including people living overseas. Nationality is held in an internal file and does not appear on the public registry certificate.

I would ask the judicial scrivener which documents are needed. I would also keep this registration requirement separate from FEFTA: completing one does not automatically complete the other.

Plan the investment process

Before treating a listing as a complete investment plan, I would ask the licensed brokerage about the property and the purchase process. I would also obtain appropriate tax and legal advice about my circumstances.

For financing, the fact-check’s practical guidance is that a buyer living abroad should plan on cash. Standard Japanese home loans generally serve people who live and earn in Japan; an available loan for a particular non-resident buyer is unverified.

The initial answer is encouraging: foreign nationality does not prevent ownership. The work is in checking the payment arrangements, registration, reporting and ongoing obligations for the actual purchase.
