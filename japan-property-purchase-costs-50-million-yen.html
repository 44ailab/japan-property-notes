Japan Property Purchase Costs for a ¥50 Million Property

For a ¥50 million property, the brokerage fee cap calculates to ¥1,560,000 before consumption tax.
A contract at exactly ¥50 million falls in the ¥10,000 reduced stamp-duty band.
The stamp-duty result changes when the contract amount goes over ¥50 million.

The detail that caught my attention at this price was the word “over” in the stamp-duty table. A boundary amount can sit in a different band from an amount just above it. I would check the actual contract amount rather than use a rounded listing price.

Calculate the brokerage ceiling first

For a property priced above ¥4,000,000, the legal cap on the brokerage fee is 3% of the price + ¥60,000 + consumption tax.

For ¥50 million, the calculation gives ¥1,560,000 before consumption tax. That is the ceiling from the stated formula. The amount charged in the transaction should be confirmed with the brokerage, including the consumption tax.

I would not combine that ceiling with an estimated total and assume the result is a firm settlement amount. The individual costs have different bases, and a ceiling is not necessarily the actual fee.

Read the stamp-duty boundary

For contracts created by March 31, 2027, the National Tax Agency’s reduced table gives these relevant bands:

- Over ¥10 million through ¥50 million: ¥10,000.
- Over ¥50 million through ¥100 million: ¥30,000.

A contract at exactly ¥50 million is in the first of those bands. A contract amount over ¥50 million is in the next one. That is why I would check the final contract amount and the date the contract is created.

Separate the land and building tax questions

The purchase price does not give me the fixed-asset-tax assessed values. Those values are the base for registration and license tax.

For land transferred by sale, the rate is reduced to 1.5% for registrations made by March 31, 2029. For a building transferred by sale, the rate is 2.0%. I would ask for the relevant assessed values and the transaction-specific calculation.

Real estate acquisition tax is a separate prefectural tax, also based on assessed value. For acquisitions made by March 31, 2027, the reduced rate is 3% for land and houses; non-residential buildings remain at 4%. The taxable base for building land is halved under the applicable rules. Deductions and reductions may be available, but the property and buyer’s circumstances need checking.

The prefecture sends a payment notice after purchase. Practitioner guidance commonly puts it around three to six months later, with timing varying by prefecture. I would make room for that later notice in the cash plan.

Use the right reference for the property

On a used condo, the closing costs commonly add up to about 6–8% of the price (SUUMO's closing-cost guide). The reference is specifically for a used condo; I would not apply it as a guaranteed result for every type of property.

A judicial scrivener’s fee also needs an actual quote. There is no official fee schedule, and offices set their own fees. The national survey average of ¥56,678 including tax describes a particular ownership-transfer example with a combined assessed value of ¥10 million. It does not establish the fee for this ¥50 million purchase.

My next step would be to collect the brokerage’s fee quote, the assessed values, and the scrivener’s quote, then ask the relevant professionals to explain the calculations and applicable reductions.

Sources: National Tax Agency: stamp duty, National Tax Agency: registration and license tax, judicial scrivener survey summary, SUUMO: condo purchase costs.
