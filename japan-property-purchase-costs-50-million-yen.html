Quick answer

For a ¥50 million property, the brokerage fee cap calculates to ¥1,560,000 before consumption tax, and a contract at exactly ¥50 million falls in the ¥10,000 reduced stamp-duty band. The stamp-duty result changes when the contract amount goes over ¥50 million, and the registration and license tax and the real estate acquisition tax are based on assessed values, not the price.

The detail that caught my attention at this price was the word “over” in the stamp-duty table. A boundary amount can sit in a different band from an amount just above it. I would check the actual contract amount rather than use a rounded listing price.

Calculate the brokerage ceiling first

For a property priced above ¥4,000,000, the legal cap on the brokerage fee is 3% of the price + ¥60,000 + consumption tax.

For ¥50 million, the calculation gives ¥1,560,000 before consumption tax. That is the ceiling from the stated formula. The amount charged in the transaction should be confirmed with the brokerage, including the consumption tax.

I would not combine that ceiling with an estimated total and assume the result is a firm settlement amount. The individual costs have different bases, and a ceiling is not necessarily the actual fee.

Read the stamp-duty boundary

For contracts created by March 31, 2027, the National Tax Agency’s reduced table gives these relevant bands:

- Over ¥10 million through ¥50 million: ¥10,000.
- Over ¥50 million through ¥100 million: ¥30,000.

A contract at exactly ¥50 million is in the first of those bands. A contract amount over ¥50 million is in the next one. That is why I would check the final contract amount and the date the contract is created.

Separate the land and building tax questions

The purchase price does not give me the fixed-asset-tax assessed values. Those values are the base for registration and license tax.

For land transferred by sale, the rate is reduced to 1.5% for registrations made by March 31, 2029. For a building transferred by sale, the rate is 2.0%. I would ask for the relevant assessed values and the transaction-specific calculation.

Real estate acquisition tax is a separate prefectural tax, also based on assessed value. For acquisitions made by March 31, 2027, the reduced rate is 3% for land and houses; non-residential buildings remain at 4%. The taxable base for building land is halved under the applicable rules. Deductions and reductions may be available, but the property and buyer’s circumstances need checking.

The prefecture sends a payment notice after purchase. Practitioner guidance commonly puts it around three to six months later, with timing varying by prefecture. I would make room for that later notice in the cash plan.

Use the right reference for the property

On a used condo, the closing costs commonly add up to about 6–8% of the price (SUUMO's closing-cost guide). The reference is specifically for a used condo; I would not apply it as a guaranteed result for every type of property.

A judicial scrivener’s fee also needs an actual quote. There is no official fee schedule, and offices set their own fees. The national survey average of ¥56,678 including tax describes a particular ownership-transfer example with a combined assessed value of ¥10 million. It does not establish the fee for this ¥50 million purchase.

My next step would be to collect the brokerage’s fee quote, the assessed values, and the scrivener’s quote, then ask the relevant professionals to explain the calculations and applicable reductions.

Same ¥50 million price, two different bills: used house vs used condo

If I were comparing a used house and a used condo at ¥50 million, I would not expect the purchase costs to be identical. Some lines start with the price, while others use the property’s fixed-asset-tax assessed value. At this price, I would also check the exact contract amount because it sits on a stamp duty boundary.

At exactly ¥50 million, the brokerage fee cap is ¥1,560,000 before consumption tax, or ¥1,716,000 including 10% consumption tax. It is a cap, not necessarily the fee charged. Stamp duty at exactly ¥50 million is ¥10,000: the reduced-rate band runs over ¥10 million through ¥50 million. For a contract amount over ¥50 million through ¥100 million, the duty is ¥30,000. These reduced rates apply to contracts created by March 31, 2027.

Registration and license tax and real estate acquisition tax use assessed value rather than the purchase price. For registration by March 31, 2029, the rate for land transferred by sale is 1.5%; the building rate is 2.0%. Acquisition tax is 3% for acquisitions by March 31, 2027, with the taxable base for residential land halved. I cannot calculate either tax from the ¥50 million price alone.

The assessed property also looks different in each case: a house is assessed as a whole land parcel plus a building, while a condo is assessed as one unit plus a share of the land. The prefecture bills acquisition tax later, commonly about three to six months after purchase, although timing varies. I would leave money aside for that notice.

I would also check what the judicial scrivener benchmark actually covers. The national survey average of ¥56,678 including tax is for an ownership transfer involving one land parcel and one building, with a combined assessed value of ¥10 million. That fits the house pattern, but it does not establish the fee for a ¥50 million purchase. For a condo, I would ask for a quote rather than assume the same amount.

Earthquake insurance is the clearest annual difference I checked. It is an add-on to fire insurance, and the chosen cover is 30–50% of the fire insurance amount, capped at ¥50 million for the building. That percentage is a cover limit, not a guaranteed payout. Per ¥10 million of cover per year, before discounts, the Tokyo/Chiba/Kanagawa/Shizuoka band charges ¥41,100 for wooden construction and ¥27,500 for non-wooden construction. In the lowest band, the figures are ¥11,200 and ¥7,300 respectively.

For a broader planning reference, SUUMO puts closing costs at about 6–8% of the price for a used condo and 5–10% for a used home, including the brokerage fee. At ¥50 million, those ranges are ¥3,000,000–¥4,000,000 and ¥2,500,000–¥5,000,000 respectively. I would use them as planning references, not quotes.

Three questions ¥50 million buyers ask

How much cash do I need on top of the price? At exactly ¥50 million, the three starting lines I can put figures on are the brokerage fee cap including consumption tax (¥1,716,000), stamp duty (¥10,000), and the judicial scrivener survey average (¥56,678). Together they come to ¥1,782,678. This is not a final bill: the brokerage figure is a cap, and the scrivener figure is a benchmark based on a different assessed value. Registration and acquisition taxes sit on top and cannot be totalled without the assessed values. SUUMO’s planning ranges at ¥50 million are ¥3,000,000–¥4,000,000 for a used condo and ¥2,500,000–¥5,000,000 for a used home, including the brokerage fee.

When is each cost paid? Stamp duty is paid on the contract, and registration and license tax is paid at the ownership-transfer registration. Acquisition tax comes later by prefectural notice, commonly about three to six months after purchase, although timing varies. I would keep money aside for that later bill.

My contract came to just over ¥50 million — does anything change? The stamp duty moves from ¥10,000 to ¥30,000 when the contract amount goes over ¥50 million, within the next reduced-rate band through ¥100 million. The brokerage cap formula does not change, although the cap should be calculated using the actual price. I would check the final contract amount rather than rely on a rounded listing price.

Frequently asked questions

How much are the closing costs when buying a ¥50 million house in Japan?

The lines fixed by the price are the brokerage fee cap of ¥1,716,000 including consumption tax, stamp duty of ¥10,000 at the reduced rate (contracts created by March 31, 2027), and the judicial scrivener fee at the national survey average of ¥56,678 including tax — ¥1,782,678 in total. Registration and license tax and real estate acquisition tax are added on top, because both are calculated on the assessed value rather than the price.

What is the brokerage fee on a ¥50 million property in Japan?

The legal cap is 3% of the price + ¥60,000 + consumption tax. At ¥50 million that is ¥1,560,000 before consumption tax, or ¥1,716,000 including it. The cap is a ceiling, not necessarily the fee quoted in a particular transaction.

How much is stamp duty on a ¥50 million property contract in Japan?

At the reduced rate, a sale contract amount over ¥10 million through ¥50 million carries ¥10,000 in stamp duty, so a contract at exactly ¥50 million is ¥10,000. The next band — over ¥50 million through ¥100 million — is ¥30,000. The reduced rates apply to contracts created by March 31, 2027, and electronic contracts carry no stamp duty.

Do non-residents have to report a property purchase in Japan?

Yes. A non-resident who acquires real property in Japan must report to the Minister of Finance via the Bank of Japan within 20 days of the acquisition. Since April 1, 2026, acquisitions are reportable regardless of purpose or amount. Not reporting, or filing a false report, carries up to six months' imprisonment or a fine of up to ¥500,000.
