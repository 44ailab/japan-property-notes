Can I Buy Property in Japan Without Visiting?

When I looked into buying Japanese property from overseas, I found useful information about payment, registration and reporting. What I did not find in my fact-check was a complete set of instructions for completing every stage without a visit. I would therefore treat the practical arrangements as questions for a licensed agent, rather than assume that a process described for one purchase will work for another.

What I could establish first

My starting point was that no permission, visa or residency is required to buy or own property in Japan. Owning property does not itself give the owner a visa or a right to stay in Japan.

That answers a question about ownership, but it does not settle how an individual purchase will be completed from abroad. I would ask a licensed agent which parts of the particular transaction can be handled while I remain overseas and what arrangements would be needed for any part that cannot.

Payment and proof of funds

The fact-check says a purchase does not require the buyer to hold a Japanese bank account. Payment is normally wired from abroad to the seller or scrivener side as the contract directs.

It also says that someone living outside Japan without a Japanese address generally cannot open an ordinary account at a Japanese bank. Bank rules can change, so I would confirm the position for my own case instead of assuming an account will be available.

For proof of funds, I found no single official checklist. Sellers and agents commonly ask for bank statements and a record of where the money came from. I would ask the licensed agent what the transaction requires and when those documents need to be provided. The fact-check gives no fixed list that I can promise will cover every purchase.

Registration and reporting

From October 5, 2026, an individual who becomes a registered owner must declare their nationality and related information as search information, with supporting proof. The requirement applies to Japanese and foreign buyers, including owners living overseas. A passport copy is given as an example of supporting proof for a non-resident. The nationality is held in an internal file and does not appear on the public registry certificate.

I would ask the licensed agent how the registration documents and supporting proof should be handled in my particular transaction. The fact-check establishes the declaration requirement, but it does not set out a complete remote registration procedure.

A separate point I would check is the post-purchase report. A non-resident who acquires real property in Japan must report the acquisition to the Minister of Finance via the Bank of Japan within 20 days. The report is in Japanese, and a Japan-resident agent, such as the brokerage, may submit it. Residence, rather than nationality, determines non-resident status for this purpose.

Questions I would take to the licensed agent

The fact-check does not give me enough detail to prescribe a complete purchase process without visiting. For a specific transaction, I would ask the licensed agent:

  • Which stages can be completed while I remain overseas, and what arrangements are required?
  • What identification and supporting documents will be needed, and how should I provide them?
  • How will the required important-matters explanation be delivered before signing?
  • How will registration be handled, including the nationality declaration and supporting proof?
  • What payment instructions will the contract give, and what proof of funds will be requested?
  • If I am a non-resident, who will prepare and submit the post-purchase report?

Before signing, a licensed agent must deliver the important-matters explanation. I would want the agent to explain how that duty will be fulfilled in my case, without assuming a method that my fact-check does not establish.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a Japanese bank account to buy?

The fact-check says a purchase does not require one. Payment is normally wired from abroad to the seller or scrivener side as the contract directs. I would confirm the arrangements for the transaction with the licensed agent.

Can I complete every purchase step without travelling to Japan?

The fact-check does not establish a complete remote procedure for every transaction. I would ask the licensed agent which stages can be handled from overseas in the particular case.

Does a non-resident buyer need to report the acquisition?

Yes. A non-resident who acquires real property in Japan must report it within 20 days to the Minister of Finance via the Bank of Japan. A Japan-resident agent may submit the Japanese-language report.
