Japan Property Purchase Costs for a ¥5 Million Property

Last reviewed: November 20, 2026

A ¥5 million purchase has costs beyond the price, and the low-price brokerage fee ceiling deserves a careful check.
For properties priced at ¥8,000,000 or less, a separate ceiling of ¥330,000 including tax applies.
For a sale contract at exactly ¥5 million, the reduced stamp duty is ¥1,000, subject to the contract rules and deadline below.

Quick answer

On a ¥5 million purchase, the brokerage fee ceiling is ¥330,000 including tax — the separate ceiling for properties priced at ¥8,000,000 or less — and the reduced stamp duty for a sale contract at exactly ¥5 million is ¥1,000. The registration and license tax is based on the fixed-asset-tax assessed value, not the purchase price, so the taxes cannot be calculated from the ¥5 million price alone.

When I started sorting the costs for a lower-priced property, I found that the headline price was the easiest figure to understand and the least useful figure to treat as the whole budget. The items around it do not all use the same base, and the brokerage fee needs particular care at this price.

Start with the brokerage quote

For a property priced above ¥4,000,000, the legal cap on the brokerage fee is 3% of the price + ¥60,000 + consumption tax.

For properties priced at ¥8,000,000 or less, a separate ceiling of ¥330,000 including tax applies.

At ¥5 million, both statements are relevant to the discussion. I would ask the brokerage for its proposed fee and the basis for the quote rather than assume that a single calculation tells me what I will be charged. A ceiling is a limit, not a promise that every brokerage will charge that amount.

Keep the tax bases separate

The registration and license tax is based on the fixed-asset-tax assessed value, not the purchase price. For a land transfer by sale, the rate is reduced to 1.5% for registrations made by March 31, 2029. The rate for a building transfer by sale is 2.0%.

That means I cannot calculate the registration tax from the ¥5 million price alone. I would need the relevant assessed values for the land and the building.

Real estate acquisition tax is another item to ask about. It is a prefectural tax based on assessed value. For acquisitions made by March 31, 2027, the reduced rate is 3% for land and houses; non-residential buildings remain at 4%. Building land has a halved taxable base within the applicable period. Deductions and reductions exist, but eligibility needs checking.

The prefecture sends the acquisition tax payment notice after purchase. Practitioner guidance commonly puts it some months later, around three to six months, but timing varies by prefecture.

A contract and a scrivener are separate costs

The National Tax Agency’s reduced stamp-duty table places a contract amount over ¥1 million through ¥5 million in the ¥1,000 band, for contracts created by March 31, 2027. Exactly ¥5 million belongs in that band; it is not the same as a contract amount over ¥5 million.

A judicial scrivener sets their own fee; there is no official tariff. In a national survey, the average for the specified ownership-transfer example was ¥56,678 including tax. That example involved one land parcel and one building with a combined assessed value of ¥10 million. It is useful context, not a quote for this property.

On a used home, buyers commonly budget about 5–10% of the price for the closing costs, including the brokerage fee (SUUMO's closing-cost guide). I would treat that as an initial planning guide, then replace it with a property-specific breakdown. A low purchase price does not tell me the assessed values, the scrivener’s quote, or which tax reductions apply.
