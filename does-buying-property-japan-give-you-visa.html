Does Buying Property in Japan Give You a Visa?

Buying property in Japan does not give you a visa or a right to live in Japan.
You do not need a Japanese visa or permanent residency simply to buy or own property.
Since October 5, 2026, a separate nationality declaration has applied to individuals becoming registered owners.

I looked for a direct connection between ownership and immigration status because it is easy to assume that buying a home or investment property creates a right to stay. The answer in the fact-check is no: Japan has no residence status for property owners, and ownership itself does not grant permission to live in the country.

The ownership question and the immigration question should therefore be researched separately. A licensed brokerage can assist with the property transaction. For a question about a particular visa or residence status, I would consult the relevant immigration authority or a qualified immigration professional.

You can own without a visa

The fact-check states that no permission, visa or residency is required simply to buy or own Japanese property. Its supporting source is secondary guidance consistent with Japan’s residence-status framework, including Housing Japan’s foreign-buyer guide.

That does not mean an owner may stay in Japan indefinitely. It means the legal ability to hold property is a different question from the permission needed for a stay.

I would be cautious about any sales message that treats the property purchase itself as an immigration route. Ask for the actual immigration basis, eligibility conditions and official guidance rather than relying on the ownership claim.

Buying and living are different plans

Someone considering an investment from overseas may intend to remain abroad. Another buyer may hope to move to Japan. In the second situation, I would not arrange the purchase on the assumption that ownership solves the residence question.

Before making commitments, I would establish what immigration status, if any, is appropriate to the planned stay. The fact-check does not verify a particular visa programme or promise that any buyer qualifies for one.

Registration does not create residence status

From October 5, 2026, an individual becoming a registered owner must declare nationality and other required search information, with supporting proof. The nationality information is held internally and does not appear on the public registry certificate.

That declaration is part of the registration process. It does not confer a visa.

A non-resident’s FEFTA report is another separate obligation. The fact-check states that the report is generally due within 20 days of acquiring Japanese real property. Residence, rather than nationality by itself, determines non-resident status for that purpose.

Questions I would separate

For the property transaction: Can I buy this property, how will payment and registration work, and what reporting is required?

For immigration: What permission is needed for my intended stay, and do I meet the relevant conditions?

For investment: What are the costs and obligations of owning the property from abroad?

Keeping those questions separate makes it harder to mistake a successful purchase for permission to live in Japan. Ownership may be possible without residency; the right to stay needs its own legal basis.
