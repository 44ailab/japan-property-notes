How Much Does Earthquake Insurance Cost for Property in Japan?

When I started looking into earthquake insurance for a property in Japan, I expected to find a single price I could use as a starting point. Instead, I found that the premium depends on where the property is and what it is made of. That changed the question I would ask: before comparing prices, I would first want to know which prefecture and structure class apply to the property.

What I found about the cost

Earthquake insurance is a government-backed add-on to fire insurance. It is not a separate figure I can assess without also thinking about the fire insurance amount.

Earthquake insurance is an add-on to fire insurance. You set the cover at 30–50% of the fire insurance amount, capped at ¥50,000,000 for the building. That percentage is the cover limit you choose — not a guaranteed payout.

The premium depends on the prefecture and structure class. The premium for the same applicable conditions is the same at every insurer. I found that useful because it gives me a way to understand what sits behind a quote: the location, the structure class and the amount of cover are details I would want to see clearly.

Looking at the published premium table

The official table in my fact-check uses an annual premium for ¥10,000,000 of cover, before discounts. For Tokyo, Chiba, Kanagawa and Shizuoka, it lists ¥41,100 for a wooden building and ¥27,500 for a non-wooden building.

The table also has a lowest band covering 28 prefectures. For that band, it lists ¥11,200 for a wooden building and ¥7,300 for a non-wooden building, again per ¥10,000,000 of cover per year before discounts.

Seeing those figures side by side helped me understand why a question about “the cost in Japan” does not lead to one useful amount. The table makes a distinction between locations and between wooden and non-wooden structures. I would use it as a starting point for questions about a particular property, with the cover amount and any applicable discounts kept in view.

What I would check before relying on a figure

I would start by asking which prefecture and structure class apply, then check the earthquake cover amount against the fire insurance amount. I would also ask how any discount is reflected in the quote, because the figures above are before discounts.

I would keep the building cover limit separate from any expectation about a payment. The 30–50% figure describes the cover selected in relation to the fire insurance amount; it does not promise a payout of that percentage.

I also found a separate cap of ¥10,000,000 for household goods. I would want a quote to make clear which amount relates to the building and which relates to household goods, rather than treating them as one figure.

The fact-check does not give an official fire insurance premium: insurers set those premiums freely, depending on the building, location and cover. So I would not use the earthquake premium table as a quote for the combined cost. For a particular property, I would ask the licensed agent to help identify the points that need to be confirmed with the insurer.

Frequently asked questions

Does earthquake insurance cost the same throughout Japan?

No. The premium depends on the prefecture and structure class. The published table shows different amounts for the Tokyo, Chiba, Kanagawa and Shizuoka band and for the lowest band.

Should I compare earthquake insurance premiums between insurers?

The earthquake insurance premium is the same at every insurer for the same applicable conditions. I would still check that the location, structure class, cover amount and any discounts being shown are understood before comparing figures.

Does the 30–50% figure mean that amount will be paid after an earthquake?

No. It is the cover limit selected as a percentage of the fire insurance amount, not a guaranteed payout. I would ask about the terms of a particular policy before relying on it.
