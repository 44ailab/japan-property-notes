Japan Rental Income Withholding Tax for a Non-Resident Owner

Rent paid to a non-resident owner is generally subject to 20.42% withholding by the payer.
An exception applies when an individual rents the property as a home for themselves or their family.
Withholding is credited in the owner’s Japanese income tax return; it is not necessarily the final tax.

When I researched rental income from a Japanese property owned overseas, I found that the tenant’s circumstances are central. The same owner should not assume every rent payment receives identical withholding treatment.

The fact-check identifies withholding for corporate tenants and business use, and an exception for an individual renting as a home for themselves or their family. I would ask a qualified Japanese tax professional to review the actual lease and payment arrangement before relying on a classification.

Who withholds?

Where the withholding obligation applies, the payer withholds from the rent. The stated rate is 20.42%. This is not a management fee and is not a guaranteed measure of the owner’s final income tax liability.

The exception in the fact-check concerns an individual tenant renting the property as a home for themselves or their family. I would confirm the tenant’s identity and intended use rather than deciding from the property’s appearance alone.

If a company is involved, or the property is used for business, I would make sure the payer and the owner’s tax adviser have reviewed the arrangement. The guidance discussing NTA rules is a starting point; the facts of the lease determine the question that needs advice.

Withholding and the tax return

The fact-check states that rental income is taxed comprehensively and the non-resident owner files a Japanese income tax return. Amounts withheld are credited in that return. Refunds are common, but I would not count on a refund or a particular outcome without a calculation for my circumstances.

For the 2027 tax year, the fact-check notes that the withheld components change while the headline rate remains 20.42%, according to the NTA. I would ask the tax adviser to use the rules applicable to the relevant tax year.

Appointing a tax agent

Before filing, a non-resident must appoint a tax agent in Japan and file the appointment with the tax office. I would arrange this with an appropriate professional and ask what records they need.

I would also ask how rent statements, withholding records and expense documentation should be organised. Keeping evidence from the start is more useful than trying to reconstruct the payments when the return is due.

Questions I would take to a tax adviser

  • Is the owner a non-resident for the relevant tax treatment?
  • Who is the payer, and does the individual home-use exception apply?
  • If withholding applies, how will the withheld amount be documented and credited?
  • Who will act as tax agent, and has the appointment been filed?
  • What records are needed for the Japanese income tax return?

Rental withholding is only one part of ownership. Fixed asset tax, management arrangements and the owner’s broader tax position are separate matters. I would have the figures reviewed for my own circumstances rather than treating the withholding rate as a forecast of investment returns.
