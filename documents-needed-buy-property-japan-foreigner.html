Documents Needed to Buy Property in Japan as a Foreigner

If I were buying property in Japan from overseas, I would start by gathering my passport and asking the agent and judicial scrivener which documents they need for my case. I would also expect to discuss a sworn statement, or affidavit, to verify my signature and overseas address if I do not have a Japanese seal-registration certificate. For a remote purchase, I would ask whether a power of attorney is needed. I would prepare records showing where my purchase funds came from, too, so the anti-money-laundering checks can be addressed early.

Start with identity and address

I would make sure my passport is valid and ask the agent and judicial scrivener what they need to verify my identity and overseas address. The judicial scrivener verifies the buyer’s identity at closing, so I would ask them how that check will work if I am buying remotely.

I would also make sure that the name and address I provide are consistent across my documents, and raise any differences with the scrivener. The aim is to find out early what needs clarification, rather than discover an issue when the transaction is ready to close.

This is a preparation guide, not an official exhaustive checklist. The documents and certification method can vary with the buyer’s circumstances, nationality, country of residence, and the judicial scrivener’s assessment.

Ask about the affidavit

Japanese residents commonly use a registered personal seal certificate, called an inkan shomeisho, in real estate registration. A foreign buyer who lives overseas generally cannot obtain that certificate or a Japanese resident record.

An affidavit, also called a sworn statement or signature certificate, is a common alternative for verifying a non-resident buyer’s signature and address. Depending on the case, it may be notarised by a notary in the buyer’s country or certified through an embassy or consulate. An apostille or other authentication may also be relevant.

I would ask the judicial scrivener which route they accept before arranging the document. I would not assume that a document prepared for another buyer, or certified in a particular place, will meet my own requirements. I would also ask whether the signature and address need to be covered in the same document.

Confirm the plan for a remote purchase

If I could not be present for the purchase, I would ask the agent and judicial scrivener whether a power of attorney is needed and what it must authorize. I would confirm who will act for me, how my identity will be checked, and how the document should be prepared and certified.

I would ask the scrivener about any document validity requirements before obtaining paperwork. The relevant validity periods have not been verified here and may depend on the document and the case.

Gather proof of funds

I would prepare clear records showing both that I have the funds and where they came from. Bank balance certificates and income records are examples of documents that may be requested for anti-money-laundering and identity checks.

I would ask the agent what records are needed for my circumstances and provide them through the requested process. Having a bank balance alone should not lead me to assume every source-of-funds question is answered; I would ask what evidence is needed to explain the origin of the money.

Questions to ask before you buy

  • Which identity and address documents do you need from me?
  • What affidavit or signature certificate will the judicial scrivener accept?
  • Where should the document be notarised or certified, and is further authentication needed?
  • If I buy remotely, do I need a power of attorney?
  • What proof-of-funds and source-of-funds records should I prepare?
  • How will the judicial scrivener verify my identity at closing?

FAQ

Can an overseas buyer use an affidavit instead of a Japanese seal-registration certificate?

An affidavit is a common alternative for a foreign non-resident buyer. The judicial scrivener should confirm the document and certification method for the case.

Will I need a power of attorney if I buy remotely?

It may be needed. Ask the agent and judicial scrivener whether it is required and what it must authorize.

Why should I prepare source-of-funds records?

They may be requested for anti-money-laundering checks. I would ask what evidence is needed early in the process.
