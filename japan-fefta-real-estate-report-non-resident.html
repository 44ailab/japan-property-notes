Japan FEFTA Real Estate Report: The 20-Day Rule for Non-Residents

※ファイル名から空白を除去：`japan-f e f t a` → `japan-f e f t a` ではなく `japan-f e f t a` の各文字間の空白を除いた `japan-fefta-real-estate-report-non-resident.html`。

A non-resident who acquires real property in Japan must generally report the acquisition within 20 days.
The report uses Form 22 and goes to the Minister of Finance through the Bank of Japan.
For ownership acquisitions on or after April 1, 2026, the reporting requirement applies regardless of purpose or amount.

When I checked the Ministry of Finance guidance, the distinction I needed first was residence, not nationality. A foreign passport by itself does not answer whether the FEFTA non-resident reporting rule applies to a particular buyer.

A Japanese citizen living abroad can be a non-resident. A foreign national living and working in Japan is generally a resident. The correct classification depends on the person’s circumstances, so I would raise it with the professional handling the transaction rather than infer it from citizenship.

What changed

The relevant amendment was effective April 1, 2026. For ownership acquisitions on or after that date, the old exemptions were removed. The fact-check states that the reporting requirement applies regardless of purpose or amount, including acquisitions for a home and acquisitions through inheritance.

Some exemptions remain for certain rights, such as particular leaseholds. I would not assume that an exemption for a right applies to an ownership purchase. If the acquisition involves something other than ownership, its classification deserves a specific check.

The report is a post-acquisition requirement. I would identify who is responsible for preparing and submitting it before the transaction is complete, because the deadline runs from acquisition.

Form, language, and submission

The report is Form 22. It is in Japanese, and a Japan-resident agent, such as the brokerage, may submit it. The report is addressed to the Minister of Finance via the Bank of Japan.

The questions I would bring to the brokerage are practical:

- Am I classified as a non-resident for this transaction?
- What acquisition date starts the reporting period?
- Who will prepare and submit Form 22?
- What information will that person need from me?
- How will I know the report has been submitted?

Those questions do not replace the official guidance or case-specific advice. They help make responsibility visible before the deadline becomes urgent.

There is a penalty for getting this wrong

Failure to report or false reporting can carry a fine of up to ¥500,000, or up to six months’ imprisonment. I would not treat the form as a routine email that can be left indefinitely for later.

There is also a separate registration requirement. From October 5, 2026, an individual becoming a registered owner must declare nationality and related information as search information, with supporting proof. That applies to Japanese and foreign buyers, including people living overseas. The nationality information is held internally and does not appear on the public registry certificate.

I keep the two items separate in my notes: FEFTA reporting concerns a non-resident acquisition, while the nationality declaration is part of the relevant ownership registration. Completing one should not be assumed to complete the other.

The Ministry of Finance’s page is the place I would return to for the reporting rules and forms. For the registration declaration, I would check the Ministry of Justice guidance and ask the professional preparing the registration.

Sources: Ministry of Finance: non-resident real-property reporting, Ministry of Justice: registration search information.
