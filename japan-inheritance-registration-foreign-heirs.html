Inheritance Registration in Japan: The 3-Year Duty for Foreign Heirs

An heir who acquires Japanese real estate by inheritance must apply to register the transfer within 3 years from learning both that the inheritance has begun and that they have acquired the property. The duty applies to foreign heirs too; I would confirm the deadline and required documents for the individual case with a judicial scrivener.

When does the registration period start?

Inheritance registration became mandatory on April 1, 2024. The 3-year period runs from the date the heir learns both that the inheritance has begun and that they have acquired the property.

I would pay attention to both parts of that description. Knowing that an inheritance has begun and learning that you have acquired a particular property are the facts to discuss when checking the deadline. I would not assume that a date in a family record, on its own, answers the question.

Without a justifiable reason, an administrative fine of up to 100,000 yen can apply. If there is uncertainty about when the period began or what counts as a justifiable reason in a particular case, I would take the facts to a judicial scrivener rather than guess.

What if the inheritance began before the duty started?

Inheritances from before April 2024 have transitional treatment. That is a reason to check the individual timeline, not a reason to assume that an older inheritance can be left unregistered.

I would gather the dates and information I have, then ask a judicial scrivener how the transitional treatment applies. If I did not know when I learned that I had acquired the property, I would say so and explain what I did know. The professional can then help identify what needs checking.

Documents for a foreign heir

Foreign heirs typically cannot use a Japanese seal certificate and instead prepare an affidavit or equivalent documents. The exact documents should be confirmed with a judicial scrivener.

I would ask that question early, especially if I were handling the matter from outside Japan. Rather than assuming that a document used by another heir will work for my case, I would explain my circumstances and ask what the scrivener needs.

It is also useful to distinguish the registration application from the work of establishing the relevant facts. I would ask which information the scrivener needs about the property and inheritance, and which documents require further preparation.

Registration and inheritance tax are separate questions

I would check the registration duty and any inheritance tax position separately. For tax, ask a licensed tax accountant how the basic exemption of 30,000,000 yen plus 6,000,000 yen per statutory heir applies to the case. Where tax is due, also ask about the 10-month filing and payment deadline.

Those figures are a starting point for a professional conversation. They do not establish whether a particular heir owes tax or what must be filed. I would give the accountant the facts of the inheritance and ask for an assessment.

Questions to ask before you buy

These questions are useful if a property you are considering involves an inheritance:

  • Has the inheritance transfer been registered, and who can confirm its status?
  • If a transfer remains to be registered, how does the deadline or transitional treatment apply?
  • What documents would a foreign heir need to prepare for the registration?
  • Should the people involved consult a licensed tax accountant about the inheritance?

FAQ

Does the registration duty apply to a foreign heir?

An heir who acquires Japanese real estate by inheritance must apply to register the transfer. A foreign heir should ask a judicial scrivener about the deadline and documents for the individual case.

Does the 3-year period always begin when the inheritance starts?

The period runs from when the heir learns both that the inheritance has begun and that they have acquired the property. Ask a judicial scrivener to assess the relevant facts and dates.

Is an affidavit always the only document a foreign heir needs?

Do not assume so. Foreign heirs typically prepare an affidavit or equivalent documents instead of a Japanese seal certificate, but the exact requirements should be confirmed with a judicial scrivener.
