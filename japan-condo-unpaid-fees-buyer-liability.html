Unpaid Condominium Fees in Japan: Does the Buyer Inherit Them?

Yes. Under Japan’s Act on Building Unit Ownership, unpaid management fees and repair-reserve contributions owed by a previous owner can pass to a buyer as a specific successor. Before signing, I would ask the licensed broker to identify any delinquency and explain the current charges and the building’s long-term repair plan.

What can pass to the buyer?

The issue is not limited to the charges a buyer expects to pay after taking ownership. Unpaid management fees and repair-reserve contributions owed by the previous owner can also pass to the buyer.

That makes the history of the unit worth checking alongside its current monthly costs. A listing can tell you enough to decide whether to ask for more information, but I would not treat it as the final word on arrears.

I would ask the licensed broker a direct question: are there unpaid management fees or repair-reserve contributions associated with this unit? If the answer is yes, I would ask the broker to explain what is owed and how it will be addressed before signing. I would also ask about arrears in the building more broadly, so I can understand what information is available.

What should the broker disclose?

The licensed broker must disclose the unit’s monthly management fee and repair reserve, any delinquency, and the building’s long-term repair plan in the statutory Important Matters Explanation before signing.

I would use that explanation as a point to check the details, not simply as a document to receive. If a fee amount differs from what I saw in a listing, or if the description of delinquency is unclear, I would ask the broker to resolve the difference before signing.

The monthly amounts and any existing delinquency answer different questions. The first tells me what charges apply to the unit; the second tells me whether there are unpaid amounts to investigate. I would ask about both explicitly.

Why check the repair plan?

The long-term repair plan is part of the information the broker must disclose. I would ask to understand its current state and how it relates to the building I am considering.

That does not mean I would try to judge the whole building from one phrase in a listing. I would ask the broker what the disclosed plan says and follow up on anything I do not understand. If information appears incomplete, I would want that made clear before signing.

I would also keep the unit and the building separate in my questions. Is there delinquency associated with this unit? Are there arrears in the building more broadly? Those questions may lead to different information, and I would not assume that an answer to one settles the other.

Questions to ask before you buy

  • What are the unit’s current monthly management fee and repair-reserve contribution?
  • Are any management fees or repair-reserve contributions owed by the previous owner, and how will any delinquency be addressed?
  • What information is available about arrears in the building?
  • What is the current state of the long-term repair plan?

FAQ

Can a buyer inherit a previous owner’s unpaid condominium fees?

Unpaid management fees and repair-reserve contributions can pass to the buyer as a specific successor. Ask the licensed broker about delinquency associated with the unit before signing.

Is the monthly fee the same issue as arrears?

No. The monthly management fee and repair-reserve contribution are current charges to confirm. Arrears are unpaid amounts that also need to be checked.

Who should explain the repair plan and delinquency?

Ask the licensed broker. The broker must disclose the specified fee, delinquency and long-term repair-plan information in the statutory Important Matters Explanation before signing.
