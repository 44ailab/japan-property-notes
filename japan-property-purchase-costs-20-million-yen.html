Japan Property Purchase Costs for a ¥20 Million Property: How Much in Fees? ¥792,678 Fixed by the Price

Direct answer: For a ¥20 million property in Japan, the three closing-cost lines that can be calculated from the price — the brokerage fee at its legal cap of ¥726,000 including consumption tax, stamp duty of ¥10,000 at the reduced rate, and the judicial scrivener fee at the national survey average of ¥56,678 including tax — come to ¥792,678. Registration and license tax and real estate acquisition tax sit on top of that and are calculated on the government's assessed value, not the price. As a planning range, buyers of used homes commonly budget about 5–10% of the price for closing costs including the brokerage fee (SUUMO's closing-cost guide).

I wanted to know which costs could actually be calculated from a ¥20 million price and which ones needed another document. That distinction turned out to be more useful than adding a generic percentage to the listing price and calling the result a quote.

What the price can tell me

For a property priced above ¥4,000,000, the legal cap on the brokerage fee is 3% of the price + ¥60,000 + consumption tax.

For ¥20 million, that calculation gives ¥660,000 before consumption tax. It is a legal ceiling, not necessarily the fee quoted in a particular transaction. I would ask the brokerage for its actual fee and the consumption tax separately so I can see the complete amount.

The contract amount can also identify the relevant stamp-duty band. Under the National Tax Agency's reduced table, a contract amount over ¥10 million through ¥50 million has ¥10,000 in stamp duty. The reduced rates apply to contracts created by March 31, 2027. The band is wide at this level: a contract at ¥11 million and a contract at ¥49 million carry the same ¥10,000, and the next band starts only above ¥50 million.

What the price cannot tell me

Registration and license tax uses the fixed-asset-tax assessed value rather than the purchase price. For land transferred by sale, the rate is reduced to 1.5% for registrations made by March 31, 2029. For a building transferred by sale, the rate is 2.0%.

I would therefore ask for the assessed value of the land and the assessed value of the building. Without them, multiplying the purchase price by those rates would give an answer using the wrong base.

Real estate acquisition tax also uses assessed value. For acquisitions made by March 31, 2027, the reduced rate is 3% for land and houses; non-residential buildings remain at 4%. Building land can have its taxable base halved under the applicable rules. Other reductions exist, and I would ask a tax professional whether they apply instead of building them into my budget automatically.

The acquisition tax notice comes from the prefecture after purchase, not at the closing table. Practitioner guidance commonly describes a wait of around three to six months, with timing varying by prefecture. I would keep that later bill on the list even if the initial settlement statement looks complete.

Use the scrivener survey carefully

The national judicial-scrivener survey gives an average of ¥56,678 including tax for an ownership-transfer example involving one land parcel and one building with a combined assessed value of ¥10 million. That assessed value is not necessarily the same thing as this page's ¥20 million purchase price.

There is no official scrivener fee schedule. Each office sets its own fees, so I would request a quote for the actual transaction and ask about document costs.

For a broader initial budget, the property type matters. On a used condo, the closing costs commonly add up to about 6–8% of the price (SUUMO's closing-cost guide). On a used home, buyers commonly budget about 5–10% of the price for the closing costs, including the brokerage fee (SUUMO's closing-cost guide). Those are planning references for used properties, not guarantees or substitutes for the individual figures above.

Frequently asked questions

How much are the closing costs when buying a ¥20 million house in Japan?

The lines fixed by the price are the brokerage fee cap of ¥726,000 including consumption tax, stamp duty of ¥10,000 at the reduced rate (contracts created by March 31, 2027), and the judicial scrivener fee at the national survey average of ¥56,678 including tax — ¥792,678 in total. Registration and license tax and real estate acquisition tax are added on top, because both are calculated on the assessed value rather than the price.

What is the brokerage fee on a ¥20 million property in Japan?

The legal cap is 3% of the price + ¥60,000 + consumption tax. At ¥20 million that is ¥660,000 before consumption tax, or ¥726,000 including it. The cap is a ceiling, not necessarily the fee quoted in a particular transaction.

How is the registration and license tax calculated in Japan?

It is charged on the fixed-asset-tax assessed value, not the purchase price. For land transferred by sale the rate is reduced to 1.5% for registrations made by March 31, 2029; for a building transferred by sale it is 2.0%. Without the assessed values for the land and the building, this tax cannot be calculated from the price.

Can foreigners buy property in Japan?

Yes. There is no visa or residency requirement to buy or own property in Japan, and no permission is required. Owning property does not itself give you a visa or a right to live in Japan.
