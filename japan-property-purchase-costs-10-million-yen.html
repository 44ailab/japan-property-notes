Japan Property Purchase Costs for a ¥10 Million Property

Last reviewed: November 20, 2026

For a ¥10 million property, the brokerage fee cap works out to ¥360,000 before consumption tax.
For a sale contract at exactly ¥10 million, the reduced stamp duty is ¥5,000, subject to the applicable deadline.

Quick answer

For a ¥10 million property, the brokerage fee cap works out to ¥360,000 before consumption tax, and the reduced stamp duty for a sale contract at exactly ¥10 million is ¥5,000. Registration and acquisition taxes use assessed values, so the purchase price alone cannot produce a reliable total.

I wanted to know which costs could actually be calculated from a ¥10 million price and which ones needed another document. That distinction turned out to be more useful than adding a generic percentage to the listing price and calling the result a quote.

What the price can tell me

For a property priced above ¥4,000,000, the legal cap on the brokerage fee is 3% of the price + ¥60,000 + consumption tax.

For ¥10 million, that calculation gives ¥360,000 before consumption tax. It is a legal ceiling, not necessarily the fee quoted in a particular transaction. I would ask the brokerage for its actual fee and the consumption tax separately so I can see the complete amount.

The contract amount can also identify the relevant stamp-duty band. Under the National Tax Agency’s reduced table, a contract amount over ¥5 million through ¥10 million has ¥5,000 in stamp duty. The reduced rates apply to contracts created by March 31, 2027. The boundary matters: exactly ¥10 million is in this band, while an amount over ¥10 million moves to the next listed band.

What the price cannot tell me

Registration and license tax uses the fixed-asset-tax assessed value rather than the purchase price. For land transferred by sale, the rate is reduced to 1.5% for registrations made by March 31, 2029. For a building transferred by sale, the rate is 2.0%.

I would therefore ask for the assessed value of the land and the assessed value of the building. Without them, multiplying the purchase price by those rates would give an answer using the wrong base.

Real estate acquisition tax also uses assessed value. For acquisitions made by March 31, 2027, the reduced rate is 3% for land and houses; non-residential buildings remain at 4%. Building land can have its taxable base halved under the applicable rules. Other reductions exist, and I would ask a tax professional whether they apply instead of building them into my budget automatically.

The acquisition tax notice comes from the prefecture after purchase, not at the closing table. Practitioner guidance commonly describes a wait of around three to six months, with timing varying by prefecture. I would keep that later bill on the list even if the initial settlement statement looks complete.

Use the scrivener survey carefully

The national judicial-scrivener survey gives an average of ¥56,678 including tax for an ownership-transfer example involving one land parcel and one building with a combined assessed value of ¥10 million. That assessed value is not necessarily the same thing as this page’s ¥10 million purchase price.

There is no official scrivener fee schedule. Each office sets its own fees, so I would request a quote for the actual transaction and ask about document costs.

For a broader initial budget, the property type matters. On a used condo, the closing costs commonly add up to about 6–8% of the price (SUUMO's closing-cost guide). That is a planning reference for a used condo, not a guarantee or a substitute for the individual figures above.

Sources: National Tax Agency: registration and license tax, National Tax Agency: stamp duty, judicial scrivener survey summary, SUUMO: condo purchase costs.
