Buying Property in Fukuoka as a Foreigner

Last reviewed: November 21, 2026

Fukuoka follows the same national buying rules as the rest of Japan — no visa or residency is required to buy or own, and ownership gives no visa.
Because most overseas buyers looking at Fukuoka plan to hold the property and rent it out, the figures I spent my time on were the running ones: management fees, the annual property taxes, and the 20.42% withholding on rent paid to a non-resident owner.
None of those figures tells you what rent a property will achieve. I will not print a yield, because no official source can give me one for a specific property.

Quick answer

A foreigner can buy property in Fukuoka under the same national rules as anywhere in Japan. For a property priced above ¥4,000,000, the legal cap on the brokerage fee is 3% of the price + ¥60,000 + consumption tax. If you will own from abroad and rent the property out, budget for a management company at about 3–5% of monthly rent, fixed asset tax at the standard rate of 1.4% of the assessed value, and a tax agent in Japan — and expect rent paid to you as a non-resident to be subject to 20.42% withholding in most tenancy setups.

I started looking at Fukuoka the way I suspect many overseas buyers do — from the listings, working backwards. The purchase itself turned out to be the simple part. The part that needed real checking was what owning from abroad actually involves year after year, so that is where this page spends its time.

The purchase itself is the national process

No permission, visa, or residency is required to buy or own, and a purchase does not require a Japanese bank account — payment is normally wired from abroad as the contract directs. Before signing, a licensed agent must deliver the important-matters explanation; that is a legal duty, not a courtesy, and it is where I confirm the property's legal status and the full fee picture.

The deposit is set by agreement — commonly 5–10% of the price. Under the standard contract terms, a buyer who cancels after paying it loses the deposit, and a seller who cancels must repay double. The exact terms are in your contract, so check them before you sign. Registration and license tax is charged on the fixed-asset-tax assessed value rather than the price — land 1.5%, building 2.0% on a sale — and real estate acquisition tax follows on assessed value too, with its bill arriving from the prefecture some months after the purchase.

Owning from abroad: the management question

I cannot collect rent, handle a leaking tap, or re-let a vacant unit from another country, so a management company is not an optional extra in my planning — it is the plan. An MLIT survey of rental management companies found that fees are charged as a percentage of rent by roughly 80% of companies, and that the most common band is 3% to under 5% of the rent, followed by 5% to under 10%. I use 3–5% of monthly rent as the planning figure for standard full management, and I ask any company I talk to exactly which tasks their percentage covers.

What I do not do is work backwards from an advertised rent to a promised return. The survey tells me what management costs; nothing official tells me what a specific Fukuoka property will rent for, or how long it will sit empty between tenants. Anyone who gives you a guaranteed yield on a specific property is giving you a number no source stands behind.

The annual taxes

Fixed asset tax is charged at the standard rate of 1.4% of the government's assessed value. It falls on whoever owns the property on January 1 each year, the assessed value is reviewed every three years, and the bills are normally paid in four instalments across the year, with the months varying by municipality. City planning tax applies only in urbanisation-promotion areas, at a rate the municipality sets up to a cap of 0.3% of the assessed value. Residential land gets a reduced taxable base — one-sixth of the assessed value for the first 200 square metres per house — which is one reason the actual bill on a house can be lower than a first estimate from the headline rate.

Rent, withholding, and the tax agent

Rent paid to a non-resident owner is subject to 20.42% withholding by the payer — except when the tenant is an individual renting the property as a home for themselves or their family, in which case no withholding applies. A corporate tenant, or a property used for business, means withholding applies.

Withholding is not the end of the story. The owner files a Japanese final return on the net rental income — the filing window is February 16 to March 15 — the withheld amounts are credited, and any excess is refunded. Before filing, a non-resident must appoint a tax agent in Japan and file the appointment with the tax office. That appointment is one of the first things I would set up after buying, not something to scramble for at filing time.

Two deadlines right after you buy

Since April 1, 2026, a non-resident who acquires real property in Japan must report to the Minister of Finance via the Bank of Japan within 20 days of the acquisition, regardless of purpose or amount. The report is in Japanese; a Japan-resident agent such as the brokerage may submit it. The penalty for not reporting or filing falsely is up to six months' imprisonment or a fine of up to ¥500,000.

And from October 5, 2026, an individual who becomes a registered owner must declare their nationality etc. as search information at registration, with supporting proof such as a passport copy for non-residents. It applies to Japanese and foreign buyers alike and does not appear on the public registry certificate.
