How to Transfer Money From Overseas to Buy Property in Japan

A buyer does not need to hold a Japanese bank account simply to purchase property.
Payment is normally wired from abroad to the seller or scrivener side as the contract directs.
There is no single official proof-of-funds checklist or fixed provider-fee table in my fact-check.

When I looked at the payment process, the most useful question was not “Which transfer service is cheapest?” It was “Who must receive each payment, in what form, and when must it arrive?” Those answers depend on the transaction and the contract.

I would obtain written payment instructions from the licensed professionals handling the purchase and confirm them through a trusted channel before sending money. I would also ask my sending bank about its requirements and the time it expects the transfer to take.

Start with the contract instructions

The fact-checked guidance says payment is normally wired from abroad to the seller or scrivener side as the contract directs. I would not assume that a bank account belonging to an introducer, or an account mentioned in an unrelated message, is the correct destination.

Ask the brokerage and scrivener to explain which payment is due to which recipient. Before initiating a transfer, independently confirm the beneficiary details using contact information you already trust. If instructions change, I would pause and verify the change directly.

This is a payment-planning step, not an invitation for an introducer to hold or move client money. The licensed professionals and the parties to the transaction should explain the contractual payment arrangements.

Ask about currency and costs

International transfer and remittance charges vary. My fact-check contains no official fee table and no verified best provider. I would compare the actual quote from my bank or chosen service and ask:

  • What currency will be sent and received?
  • What exchange rate and charges apply to this transfer?
  • Are any intermediary or receiving charges expected?
  • How much must arrive for the contractual payment to be satisfied?
  • What information or supporting documents does the sending institution require?

I would avoid relying on a headline exchange rate without checking the full quote and the amount the recipient is expected to receive.

Prepare for proof-of-funds questions

There is no single official checklist in the fact-check. In practice, sellers and agents commonly ask for bank statements and a record of where the money came from. Banks also have statutory anti-money-laundering obligations.

That does not mean every buyer will be asked for the same documents. I would ask the brokerage and the sending bank what they need for my case, when they need it and how to submit it securely. If the funds come from more than one source, I would explain that early and ask what records are appropriate.

A Japanese account is a separate issue

A person living outside Japan without a Japanese address generally cannot open an ordinary account at a Japanese bank. Bank rules can change, so confirm eligibility for your circumstances. The ability to buy does not itself mean an ordinary account will be available.

I would plan the purchase payment and future ownership payments separately. Ask the relevant professionals how taxes, management charges and other expenses will be handled after acquisition; the purchase wire alone does not answer those questions.

My working checklist is simple: confirm the contractual recipient, verify the instructions, obtain a complete transfer quote, prepare the evidence requested and allow for the bank’s stated processing requirements.

Do I need a Japanese bank account to send the purchase money?

The guidance I reviewed suggests that a Japanese account is not necessarily required for the purchase. I would ask the licensed brokerage how payment is to be made for the specific transaction before arranging a transfer.

What should I confirm before sending money?

I would confirm the recipient’s account details through a trusted channel, along with the currency, amount and payment deadline. I would also ask both the sending and receiving institutions about fees and processing requirements, because I cannot assume those will be the same in every case.

What documents might the bank ask for?

In the sources I checked, bank statements and information about the source of funds came up as possible requests. I could not find a single document checklist that applies to every buyer, so I would ask the institutions involved before the payment deadline.

Can I assume the recipient will receive exactly what I send?

I would not assume that without checking the currency conversion and any fees with the institutions involved. The useful question to ask is how much the recipient must receive and what the sender needs to do to meet that amount.
