Mortgage in Japan for Non-Resident Investors

If you live outside Japan and want to buy Japanese investment property, I would plan on a cash purchase. There is no official Japanese mortgage program for people who live outside Japan, and standard Japanese home loans are intended for people who live and earn in Japan.

Some lenders serve foreign residents, but “foreigner-friendly” does not mean available to non-residents. Before counting on any financing, I would ask the brokerage and the lender directly whether they can serve someone living in my country and buying an investment property.

Why resident mortgage information can be misleading

When I look at Japanese mortgage information aimed at foreigners, I check whether it is aimed at people who already live and work in Japan. Lenders such as SMBC Trust Bank PRESTIA serve foreign residents; that does not establish that they lend to buyers living abroad.

Published-style conditions for resident borrowers can include a work visa, income in Japan of roughly ¥5,000,000 or more, and a down payment of around 20–30%. Those are not terms I would assume apply to a non-resident.

How I would plan the purchase

I would start by asking whether I can fund the purchase in cash, then ask the brokerage for an itemized estimate of the purchase costs. I would also ask a qualified tax professional about my obligations in Japan and in my country of residence.

If financing is important to the decision, I would check options with lenders directly before committing to a property. I would ask them to confirm eligibility and terms for my residence, income and intended investment use. I would not treat a general mortgage page as confirmation.

Questions to ask before relying on a loan

I would put these questions to the lender and brokerage:

  • Does the lender accept applications from buyers who live in my country?
  • Does it finance Japanese property bought for investment?
  • What documents and evidence of income would it require?
  • What down payment, fees and other conditions would apply to my application?

Until the lender confirms the answers, I would treat financing as unverified.

FAQ

Can a non-resident get a standard Japanese home loan?

Standard Japanese home loans are intended for people who live and earn in Japan. I would not assume one is available for an overseas buyer or an investment purchase.

Does a foreigner-friendly lender serve buyers who live abroad?

Not necessarily. A lender may serve foreign residents of Japan without serving non-residents. I would ask the lender directly about my circumstances.

Should I plan to buy with cash?

Yes. If I lived abroad, I would plan on a cash purchase and treat any potential financing as unverified until a lender confirmed it.
