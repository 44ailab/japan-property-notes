Japan's 2027 Tax Surtax Change: Do Foreign Owners Pay More?

Last reviewed: November 21, 2026

The headline reached me the way tax headlines usually do — as a warning. A new defence surtax was coming in 2027, and my first reading was that a foreign owner's withholding bill was about to rise. I went back to the rate breakdown I had already verified, and the alarm did not survive the arithmetic. What changes from 2027 is the composition of the surtaxes inside the rates — which named surtaxes the total is built from. The combined rates a foreign property owner actually pays are unchanged. This page is the short version of that check, so the next headline costs you less worry than the first one cost me.

Quick answer

No — on the verified figures, the combined rates do not change. Rent withholding for a non-resident owner stays at 20.42%. Non-resident capital gains stay at 30.63% (held five years or less) and 15.315% (held over five years). The buyer withholding on a non-resident's sale stays at 10.21%. From the 2027 tax year, the components inside those totals are re-arranged — a defence surtax is added to the mix and the reconstruction surtax's share changes — while the headline totals stay the same.

What actually changes: the labels inside the total

The rates above are built from national income tax plus surtaxes. The reconstruction special income tax has been one of those components since the earthquake reconstruction period. From 2027, the composition is re-arranged: a defence surtax enters the structure and the reconstruction component's share shrinks, in a way that leaves the combined percentages where they were. The National Tax Agency's own note on the rent withholding states it plainly in structure if not in headline: from the 2027 tax year the withheld components change while the headline rate stays 20.42%. The same pattern — composition changes, combined rate unchanged — is recorded for the non-resident capital gains rates.

What this does not change

Everything mechanical stays where it was. Rent paid to a non-resident owner is still withheld by the payer at 20.42%, with the same exception — no withholding when the tenant is an individual renting the property as a home for themselves or their family — and withholding is still not the final tax: the owner files a Japanese return, through a tax agent appointed in Japan, and the withheld amount is credited. A non-resident selling property still has 10.21% of the price withheld by the buyer (with its own narrow exception for an individual buying a home for themselves or family at ¥100,000,000 or less), credited on the final return. The deadlines, the forms, and the rates are the ones already on this hub's withholding and capital-gains pages; 2027 renames parts of the arithmetic without moving the totals.

Why I am writing this in November 2026

Because the change is exactly the kind that gets misread twice: once by headlines that report a new surtax without the unchanged total, and once by owners who then budget for a rise that is not coming. If a 2027 change beyond this composition shift is enacted, this page's figures would need re-checking — I flag that openly rather than presenting a November 2026 verification as permanent. What I can say on the verified record is narrow and, for planning, sufficient: the surtax restructure changes what the rates are made of, not what they add up to.
