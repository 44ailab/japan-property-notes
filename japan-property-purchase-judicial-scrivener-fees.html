Japan Judicial Scrivener Fees When Buying Property: How Much? The ¥56,678 National Average

A national survey found an average ownership-transfer registration fee of ¥56,678, including tax, for its specified case.
That survey case covered one land parcel and one building with a combined assessed value of ¥10,000,000.
There is no official fee schedule: each judicial scrivener sets their own fees.

When I searched for a judicial scrivener fee to put into a Japanese property budget, I wanted a number I could rely on. What I found was more limited: a useful survey benchmark, but not a tariff that every office must follow.

A judicial scrivener, or shiho-shoshi, handles registration work. The fee for that professional work should be distinguished from registration and license tax and from document costs. An estimate that combines everything into one line makes it harder to compare quotes or understand the cash required.

What the survey actually measured

The Japan Federation of Shiho-shoshi Associations’ survey was conducted in March 2024 and sampled 5,558 scriveners. For ownership-transfer registration on a sale, its specified case was one land parcel and one building with a combined assessed value of ¥10,000,000.

The average fee for that case was ¥56,678, including tax. I would describe that as around ¥57,000 on average in the national survey, plus small document costs; your quote varies by office and case.

The description of the survey case matters. The assessed value in the survey is not a statement that the property was purchased for the same amount. The average also does not tell you that any particular scrivener will charge that amount.

The published discussion of the survey is useful when asking what a benchmark includes. For a purchase, though, I would request a quote for the actual work.

Ask for separate lines

I would ask the scrivener or the licensed brokerage coordinating the transaction to show:

  • The scrivener’s professional fee, including whether consumption tax is included.
  • Registration and license tax separately.
  • Document and other incidental costs.
  • Any work outside the quoted ownership-transfer registration.
  • What information or documents are needed from an overseas buyer.

The national survey also reported ¥42,699 for mortgage registration in its surveyed case. I would not add that figure automatically to a cash purchase or assume it is the quote for a financed transaction. Ask whether mortgage registration is needed and what the office will charge for the actual case.

Why a quote can differ

There is no official fee schedule. Each office sets its fees, and the survey benchmark describes a particular set of facts. A real purchase may involve different property details or additional work. Rather than treating a difference from the survey average as an error, I would ask the office to explain the scope of its quote.

Registration tax is a separate question. For example, the tax base for a transfer by sale is the fixed-asset-tax assessed value, with different rates for land and buildings. That tax is not the scrivener’s professional income.

When to ask

I would request an itemised estimate early enough to include it in the purchase budget, then confirm it when the necessary property and registration details are available. If the estimate changes, ask which line changed and why.

The survey gives me a reference point. The written quote gives me a basis for planning my own transaction.
