Japan Stamp Duty on a Real Estate Sale Contract

Last reviewed: November 21, 2026

Stamp duty is the smallest line in a Japanese property purchase, and it was still the one I got wrong first. I had read a single figure — the duty for one contract size — and assumed it scaled smoothly with the price. It does not. The duty is a table of bands, the band edges are exact, and a reduced table currently applies to contracts created by March 31, 2027. When I sat down to check the whole table against the National Tax Agency's figures, I found my contract fell into a different band from the one I had budgeted for. This page is the full table, so you do not have to reconstruct it from fragments the way I did.

Quick answer

In Japan, stamp duty on a real estate sale contract is set by the contract amount, in bands. Under the reduced rates for contracts created through March 31, 2027, a contract over ¥10 million through ¥50 million carries ¥10,000; over ¥50 million through ¥100 million carries ¥30,000; over ¥100 million through ¥500 million carries ¥60,000. A contract concluded electronically carries no stamp duty at all. The full standard and reduced table is below.

Two details in the table caught me out, and both are worth stating plainly. The reduction applies only to contract amounts above ¥100,000 — the two smallest taxable bands are ¥200 either way. And the band edges are strict: a contract at exactly ¥10 million sits in the ¥5,000 reduced band, while a contract ¥1 above it moves to ¥10,000. When a price is being negotiated near an edge, the duty difference is small money, but I would rather know it before signing than after.

The electronic-contract exemption

The duty attaches to the paper document. A sale contract concluded electronically — exchanged and signed as electronic data rather than as a stamped paper original — carries no stamp duty. I checked this carefully because it sounded like a loophole; it is simply how the tax is defined. Whether a given transaction uses paper or electronic contracts is a matter for the parties and the brokerage, and the rest of the contract's terms are unaffected — but on a large contract the difference between the paper duty and zero is worth a question before the format is chosen.

Where stamp duty sits in the whole purchase

Stamp duty is minor next to the costs that actually shape a budget. For a property priced above ¥4,000,000, the legal cap on the brokerage fee is 3% of the price + ¥60,000 + consumption tax — on a ¥20 million property, ¥660,000 before consumption tax, against ¥10,000 of stamp duty on the same contract. Registration and license tax uses the fixed-asset-tax assessed value rather than the purchase price — land transferred by sale is reduced to 1.5% for registrations made by March 31, 2029, and a building transferred by sale is 2.0% — and real estate acquisition tax, also on assessed value at a reduced 3% for land and houses for acquisitions made by March 31, 2027, arrives by prefectural notice some months after the purchase. I keep stamp duty on the checklist because it is exact and knowable, not because it is large.

Sources: National Tax Agency: reduced stamp duty rates for real-estate transfer contracts, National Tax Agency: registration and license tax.
