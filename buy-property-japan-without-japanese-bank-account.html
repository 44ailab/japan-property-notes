Can You Buy Property in Japan Without a Japanese Bank Account?

A buyer does not need to hold a Japanese bank account to purchase property in Japan.
Payment is normally wired from abroad to the seller or scrivener side as the contract directs.
For a non-resident, the FEFTA acquisition report is generally due within 20 days.

When I looked into the bank-account question, I found it helped to separate three issues: whether a Japanese account is required for the purchase, whether a particular bank will open one, and how the agreed payment will actually be made.

The first answer is the clearest: a purchase does not require the buyer to hold a Japanese bank account. That does not mean I would send money based on a generic online guide. The payment destination and procedure need to follow the contract and the instructions confirmed for the transaction.

Opening an account is a different question

In general, someone who lives outside Japan and has no Japanese address cannot open an ordinary account at a Japanese bank. Bank rules can change and eligibility depends on the bank and the individual case.

The example in my fact-check is Japan Post Bank: the cited account-opening guidance describes requirements including residence in Japan and a residence card. I would confirm current requirements directly with any bank I was considering, instead of assuming that buying property creates account eligibility.

This is also separate from the right to buy. Japan does not require a visa, permission, or residency merely to buy or own property. Ownership itself does not grant a visa or a right to live in Japan.

Plan the transfer with the people handling the transaction

Payment is normally wired from abroad to the seller or scrivener side as the contract directs. Before arranging a transfer, I would ask the licensed brokerage and the relevant professionals to explain the agreed payment instructions and timing.

I would also ask my bank or transfer provider about its process and current charges. There is no official fee table for international wires or remittance services in the fact-check, and provider prices change. I cannot give a reliable provider fee or name a cheapest option from the supplied information.

Proof of funds is another practical question. Sellers and agents commonly ask for bank statements and a record showing where the money came from. There is no single official checklist for every transaction, so I would ask what the parties need for the particular purchase rather than assume a fixed list.

Do not confuse payment with financing

A Japanese bank account is not the same issue as a mortgage. The available lender guidance in the fact-check says standard Japanese home loans are generally for people who live and earn in Japan. A buyer who lives abroad should plan on cash and confirm their own circumstances; the fact-check does not establish an available mortgage program for non-residents.

Keep reporting on the checklist

If the buyer is a non-resident for FEFTA purposes, acquisition of Japanese real property generally requires a report within 20 days. Residence, not nationality, determines the relevant classification. The report is in Japanese, and a Japan-resident agent may submit it.

I would ask the brokerage who will handle that report and what information they need. The payment route answers how the purchase money moves; it does not settle the separate reporting question.

Sources: Ministry of Finance: non-resident real-property reporting, Wise: Japan Post Bank account-opening guidance, Housing Japan: foreign ownership and residence.

Can I buy without opening a Japanese bank account?

The guidance I reviewed indicates that a Japanese account is not necessarily required for a purchase. I would confirm the payment route and receiving details for the specific transaction with the licensed brokerage and the institutions handling the funds.

Can I buy without coming to Japan?

I cannot say that every purchase can be completed remotely: the steps and documents depend on the transaction and the parties involved. I would ask the licensed brokerage which steps require my presence and whether any can be handled by an authorized representative.

Can someone living abroad open an ordinary Japanese bank account?

The bank-account guidance I found suggests that someone living abroad without a Japanese address generally cannot open an ordinary account. Rules and available account types can differ, so I would check eligibility directly with the bank rather than assume the same answer applies everywhere.

What should I ask if I plan to pay from overseas?

I would ask who will receive each payment, which currency to send, what deadline applies and how the recipient’s account details will be confirmed. I would also ask the bank and brokerage what documents they need and whether any transfer fees could affect the amount received.
