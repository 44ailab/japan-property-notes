Filing a Final Tax Return in Japan as a Non-Resident Property Owner

Last reviewed: November 21, 2026

Quick answer

A non-resident owner with rental income files a Japanese final return on net rental income. Tax withheld from rent is credited on the return, and any excess is refunded; withholding is not the final tax. A non-resident who must file or pay appoints a Japan-resident tax agent and notifies the competent tax office.

Start with net rental income

When I look at a rental statement, I separate the rent received from the final tax calculation. The stated final return is on net rental income.

The filing period for the rental income return is February 16 through March 15. I would put those dates on a calendar and gather the rental and withholding records for the tax agent before the filing period.

The verified facts here do not specify which expenses a particular owner may deduct. I would ask a Japanese tax professional to check those items rather than assume that every cost shown on a statement is deductible.

Credit rental withholding on the return

Where rental withholding applies, the payer withholds 20.42% of gross rent. That is a withholding calculation; it is not the final tax on the owner’s rental income.

The amount withheld is credited on the final return. If the withholding exceeds the amount due, the excess is refunded. I would therefore retain evidence of the amount withheld rather than treating the deduction from a payment as the end of the process.

There is an exemption when the payer is an individual renting for their own or a relative’s residence. Corporate tenants always withhold. For the payer’s rules, see the related page: Japan Rental Income Withholding Tax for a Non-Resident Owner.

Appoint a tax agent in Japan

A non-resident who must file or pay in Japan appoints a Japan-resident tax agent and notifies the competent tax office. The Japanese term is 納税管理人.

I would identify the tax agent early and make sure they have the records needed to check the return. The appointment and notification are part of the filing process; they are separate from calculating rental income and crediting withholding.

The correct tax office and the documents needed for an individual case should be checked with the tax agent.

If you also sell property

A sale can create a separate withholding credit. Where the sale withholding rule applies, the buyer withholds 10.21% of the gross price. The seller credits it on the final return, and an excess is refunded.

An exception applies when the buyer is an individual buying for their own or a relative’s residence and the price is no more than ¥100 million. I would tell the tax agent about the sale and provide the withholding records alongside the rental information.

For the buyer’s obligation and exception, see the related page: Japan Property Sale Withholding Tax for a Non-Resident Seller.

FAQ

Is rent withholding the final tax?

No. It is credited on the final return, and any excess is refunded.

Does every individual tenant withhold from rent?

No. An individual payer renting for their own or a relative’s residence is exempt from the stated rental withholding rule.

Can sale withholding also be credited?

Yes. Sale withholding is credited on the seller’s final return, and an excess is refunded.
