Can You Buy Property in Japan Without Permanent Residency?

Yes. Permanent residency is not required simply to buy or own property in Japan.
For non-residents, a FEFTA report is generally due within 20 days of acquisition.
From October 5, 2026, an individual becoming a registered owner must provide nationality and other required search information.

When I investigated this question, I found it useful to separate permission to own from the practical arrangements needed to complete a purchase. The first answer is straightforward: a foreign buyer does not need Japanese permanent residency merely to hold property. The practical answers depend on where the buyer lives and how the purchase will be funded.

Buying property also does not create a visa or a right to live in Japan. If you want to reside in Japan, assess immigration eligibility separately.

Your residence can affect reporting

Permanent residency and FEFTA non-resident status are not interchangeable labels. The fact-check says FEFTA non-resident status is determined by residence, not nationality alone. A Japanese citizen living abroad can be a non-resident, while a foreign national living and working in Japan is generally a resident.

The Ministry of Finance’s guidance describes the post-acquisition report for non-residents. The report is generally due within 20 days, is made on Form 22 and is in Japanese. A Japan-resident agent, such as a brokerage, may submit it.

I would ask an appropriate professional to confirm my status, identify who will submit the report and provide evidence of filing. Failure to report or false reporting can carry a fine of up to ¥500,000, or up to six months’ imprisonment.

Registration has its own requirements

From October 5, 2026, an individual becoming a registered owner must declare nationality and other required search information, with supporting proof. Japanese and foreign buyers are included, as are individuals living overseas. Nationality is held in an internal file and does not appear on the public registry certificate.

I would ask the judicial scrivener which documents are required. The declaration is separate from FEFTA reporting and does not grant an immigration status.

Consider the bank account and payment route

A buyer does not need a Japanese bank account simply to purchase property. In practice, someone living outside Japan without a Japanese address generally cannot open an ordinary Japanese bank account. Eligibility rules can change, so check the bank’s requirements for your case.

The fact-check says payment is normally wired from abroad to the seller or scrivener side as the contract directs. I would confirm the recipient and instructions with the licensed professionals handling the transaction and check the sending bank’s documentation requirements.

Do not assume a mortgage is available

The fact-check’s practical guidance is that buyers living abroad should plan on cash. Standard Japanese home loans are generally designed for people who live and earn in Japan. The availability of a particular loan to a particular non-resident buyer is unverified.

If financing is essential, I would seek written confirmation of eligibility and terms before relying on it. I would not treat the absence of a permanent-residency requirement for ownership as evidence of mortgage approval.

The property can be legally available to someone without permanent residency while banking, financing and reporting still require planning. Those are the questions I would resolve with the appropriate institutions and professionals for the actual purchase.
