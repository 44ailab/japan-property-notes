Fixed-Term Lease vs Ordinary Lease in Japan: A Landlord's Guide

If I were buying a tenanted property in Japan, I would check the lease type before assuming I could sell it vacant later. An ordinary residential lease gives tenants strong renewal protection, while a properly established fixed-term lease ends without automatic renewal. Fixed-term terms can make the end of the tenancy clearer, but they come with strict formalities and can narrow the tenant pool. The existing lease and its paperwork are therefore part of the investment I would ask a professional to review.

What an ordinary lease means for an owner

An ordinary residential lease is called futsu shakuya. Under this type of agreement, the owner generally needs justifiable cause to refuse renewal or seek termination. The end of the stated contract period does not, by itself, mean the tenant must leave.

That matters if your plan depends on selling the property vacant or changing how it is used. Wanting to sell vacant is not, by itself, a guarantee that an owner can refuse renewal or remove a paying tenant.

A request for vacant possession can involve strict legal assessment. The owner’s need to use the property and an offer of relocation compensation may be considered, but compensation is a supporting factor, not an automatic solution. I would take the actual lease and circumstances to a qualified professional rather than rely on an assumed move-out date.

How a fixed-term lease differs

A fixed-term residential lease is called teiki shakuya. It has no automatic renewal and ends when the agreed term expires. If the owner and tenant want another lease, they need to agree to a new contract.

For an investor thinking about a future sale or change of use, that can make the end of the tenancy easier to plan around than an ordinary lease. I would still want a professional to review the documents and the applicable procedures before treating the contract date as a dependable exit plan.

The distinction also matters when buying a property that already has a tenant. I would ask what kind of lease is in place and have its paperwork reviewed, rather than assume the arrangement can simply be changed after purchase.

The advance explanation matters

A fixed-term lease requires more than a label in the contract. The agreement must be made in writing or through a permitted electronic record. Before the contract is concluded, the tenant must also receive and be given an explanation of a separate document stating that the lease will not renew and will end when the term expires.

If the required formalities are defective, the no-renewal provision may not be effective and the arrangement may be treated as an ordinary lease. That could change the exit the owner thought they had secured.

I would ask a qualified professional to check the contract and the separate advance explanation. I would also ask about any applicable end-of-term notice requirements; their timing is not verified here.

The tenant-pool trade-off

A fixed-term lease can offer clearer timing for the owner, but some prospective tenants may prefer an arrangement with renewal protection. That can narrow the tenant pool and may affect the rent the property can achieve.

I would weigh that trade-off with the licensed agent or a property professional using the circumstances of the property. The lease type affects both the planned exit and the task of finding a tenant.

Questions to ask before you buy

  • Is the existing residential lease ordinary or fixed-term?
  • If it is fixed-term, were the contract and separate advance explanation completed properly?
  • What procedures or notices would apply when the term ends?
  • If I wanted to sell vacant, what restrictions could the current lease create?
  • How might a fixed-term lease affect demand from prospective tenants?

FAQ

Can I refuse to renew an ordinary lease because I want to sell vacant?

A desire to sell vacant does not by itself guarantee that you can refuse renewal. The justifiable-cause standard applies.

Does a fixed-term lease renew automatically?

No. It ends when the agreed term expires. A new lease requires both parties’ agreement.

What if the fixed-term formalities were not completed properly?

The no-renewal provision may not be effective, and the arrangement may be treated as an ordinary lease. A qualified professional should review the paperwork.
