Japan Property Nationality Declaration at Registration for Foreign Buyers

From October 5, 2026, an individual becoming a registered owner must declare nationality and other required search information.
The requirement applies to Japanese and foreign buyers, including owners living overseas.
Nationality is held in an internal file and does not appear on the public registry certificate.

When I looked into the nationality declaration, I wanted to know two things: who must provide it, and whether it becomes public. The Ministry of Justice guidance distinguishes the required search information from what appears on a public registry certificate.

The requirement applies when an individual becomes a registered owner through ownership preservation or transfer registration. Supporting proof is required; the fact-check gives a passport copy as an example for a non-resident. I would ask the judicial scrivener handling the registration which documents are needed for my case.

This is a registration requirement

The nationality declaration is connected to registration. It is not an application for a visa, and owning property does not itself provide a right to live in Japan.

I would make the declaration part of my registration preparation: identify who is handling the application, ask what information must be supplied, and confirm the form of supporting evidence before the documents are finalised.

The requirement is not limited to foreign nationals. Japanese buyers are included, as are individuals living overseas. That makes it important to ask about the process based on the registration being made, rather than assuming it does or does not apply because of a buyer’s nationality.

What happens to the information?

The nationality information is held in an internal file. It does not appear on the public registry certificate.

That does not mean there is no obligation to provide accurate information and supporting proof. I would distinguish the submission requirement from public display: the fact that nationality is not printed on the public certificate does not remove the registration step.

If privacy or document handling is a concern, I would ask the scrivener how the required material will be submitted and retained. I would not assume that an ordinary email attachment is the only available method; the appropriate process should be confirmed with the professional handling the case.

Do not confuse it with the FEFTA report

For a non-resident acquiring Japanese real property, a separate FEFTA post-acquisition report is generally required within 20 days. Residence, not nationality alone, determines non-resident status for that reporting framework.

The nationality declaration and FEFTA report have different purposes and procedures. Completing one should not be assumed to complete the other. I would ask the brokerage or an appropriate professional to identify who is responsible for each task and what evidence of completion I will receive.

My registration checklist

Before proceeding, I would ask:

  • Does my planned registration require the nationality and other search information?
  • What supporting proof is needed for me?
  • Who will prepare and submit the registration documents?
  • What should I provide if I live outside Japan?
  • Is a separate FEFTA report required, and who will handle it?

The declaration is a practical document-preparation issue. The useful next step is to confirm the requirements with the professional handling the registration, while keeping it distinct from residence status, public registry information and any separate post-acquisition reporting.
