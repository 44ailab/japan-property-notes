Japan Property Deposit: How Much Do You Pay? 5–10% of the Price Is the Norm

The deposit is set by agreement — commonly 5–10% of the price.
If the seller is a licensed brokerage, it may not take more than 20%.
The contract’s cancellation terms matter as much as the amount.

When I looked into Japanese purchase deposits, the percentage was the easy part to find. The more consequential question was what happens after the money is paid. A deposit is not merely a budgeting line: the contract determines how it is treated and what happens if a party cancels.

The deposit is set by agreement — commonly 5–10% of the price. Under the standard contract terms, a buyer who cancels after paying it loses the deposit, and a seller who cancels must repay double. The exact terms are in your contract, so check them before you sign.

A common range is not a fixed amount

The fact-check does not identify a fixed legal deposit amount for every property purchase. The agreed amount should be clear in the contract.

The separate 20% limit stated above applies when the seller is a licensed brokerage. I would not assume that every seller falls into that category or that the limit is the amount a buyer should pay. Ask the licensed agent handling the transaction to identify the seller and explain the applicable terms.

Read the cancellation clause

I would ask the brokerage to explain, before signing:

  • What amount is due and when?
  • Who receives it under the contract?
  • How is it applied to the purchase?
  • What cancellation provisions apply to the buyer and seller?
  • Are there other conditions in the contract that affect cancellation or repayment?

The standard practice described in the fact-check is a starting point for questions, not a substitute for the actual terms. A short answer such as “the deposit is refundable” or “the deposit is non-refundable” may leave out the conditions that determine the outcome.

Hear the important-matters explanation

Before signing, a licensed agent must deliver the important-matters explanation. The fact-check identifies this as a legal duty under the Building Lots and Buildings Transaction Business Act.

I would use that stage to ask questions and request clarification from the licensed professional. An introducer should not replace the agent’s explanation or interpret an individual contract for the buyer.

If I cannot understand a provision that matters to my decision, I would seek an appropriate translation or independent professional advice before signing. The key is to understand the agreed terms while there is still an opportunity to discuss them.

Budget for the whole transaction

The deposit is part of the contractual payment arrangements; it is not a complete estimate of purchase costs. I would also ask for an itemised estimate of the other amounts due, including any brokerage fee, registration tax, scrivener’s fee and stamp duty where applicable.

I would keep the payment schedule beside the cost estimate. A budget needs to show not only how much money is required, but when and to whom it must be paid. Confirm any transfer instructions through a trusted channel before sending funds.

The common 5–10% range helps me frame the question. The signed agreement determines the terms I need to understand.

How much is the deposit?

The deposit is set by agreement — commonly 5–10% of the price. I would check the amount and payment terms in the contract rather than assume the common range is a fixed requirement.

Is the deposit the same as the other upfront costs?

When I looked at the purchase process, I found that the deposit is a payment under the sale agreement, while items such as taxes and professional fees are separate costs to check. I would ask for an itemized payment schedule so I can see what is due, when and to whom.

What happens if either side cancels after the deposit is paid?

Under standard contract terms, a buyer who cancels after paying it loses the deposit, and a seller who cancels must repay double. The exact terms are in your contract, so I would check them before signing rather than assume every cancellation is treated the same way.

Is the deposit amount fixed by law?

The sources I reviewed describe the amount as a matter of agreement, not one fixed amount for every purchase. There are legal limits in some circumstances, so I would ask the licensed brokerage to explain the terms that apply to the particular sale.
