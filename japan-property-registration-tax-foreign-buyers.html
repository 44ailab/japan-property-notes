Japan Property Registration Tax for Foreign Buyers

Registration tax is calculated from the fixed-asset-tax assessed value, not the purchase price.
For a land transfer by sale registered by March 31, 2029, the reduced rate is 1.5%.
For a building transfer by sale, the rate in the fact-checked schedule is 2.0%.

When I first compared Japanese property purchase costs, I found it easy to confuse the price agreed with the seller and the value used for registration tax. They are not necessarily the same. That distinction matters more than trying to calculate the tax directly from a listing.

The National Tax Agency’s registration and license tax schedule sets out rates for different kinds of registration. The figures above concern transfers by sale; a different registration can have a different treatment.

Find the assessed value first

For this tax, the relevant base is the fixed-asset-tax assessed value. I would ask the licensed professionals handling the purchase for the assessed values used for the land and building and for an itemised estimate.

The land and building rates should not be blended into a single percentage of the purchase price. If a purchase includes both, ask to see each part of the calculation. A listing price alone does not give you the assessed values you need.

Land and building are treated separately

For land transferred by sale, the base rate is 2.0%, reduced to 1.5% for registrations made by March 31, 2029.

For a building transferred by sale, the rate in the fact-check is 2.0%. A lower 0.3% rate exists for an individual buying a home to live in, subject to conditions and the stated March 31, 2027 deadline. I would not assume that relief applies to an investment purchase. Ask the professional preparing the registration whether any relief is available in your particular case.

The deadline attached to the land reduction concerns the registration date. When a transaction is approaching a deadline, I would ask the scrivener which date and rate will apply instead of assuming that the contract date decides the issue.

Registration tax and the scrivener’s fee

The tax and the judicial scrivener’s professional fee are different lines in the budget. A scrivener can prepare and handle registration work; the registration tax is a tax. The scrivener’s own quote may also identify document costs.

There is no official fee schedule for scriveners. A national survey found an average of around ¥57,000, including tax, for a specified ownership-transfer registration case. Your quote can differ because the survey described a particular case, not every purchase.

What I would request before committing

I would ask for an estimate showing:

  • The land and building assessed values used.
  • The registration category and rate applied to each.
  • Any claimed relief and the reason it is available.
  • The scrivener’s fee and document costs separately from the tax.
  • Whether the calculation may change when the registration documents are prepared.

Nationality is also a separate registration issue. From October 5, 2026, an individual becoming a registered owner must declare nationality and other required search information with supporting proof. The nationality information is held internally and does not appear on the public registry certificate. It is not a substitute for the tax calculation.

A good estimate tells you which values and rates produced the figure. If it gives only a total, I would ask for the breakdown before putting that total into my purchase budget.
