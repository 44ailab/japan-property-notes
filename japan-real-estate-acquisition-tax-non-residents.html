Japan Real Estate Acquisition Tax for Non-Residents

Real estate acquisition tax is a one-time prefectural tax based on assessed value.
The reduced rate is 3% for land and houses acquired by March 31, 2027; non-residential buildings remain at 4%.
The prefecture sends a payment notice after purchase, so the bill may not be present at closing.

When I looked into the costs that follow a Japanese property purchase, the timing of acquisition tax stood out. It is possible to focus on the money needed for settlement and overlook a separate notice that arrives later.

The tax is based on the assessed value, not automatically on the price paid to the seller. Land and buildings also need separate attention because their classifications and relief can differ.

Start with the property and the assessed value

The standard rate is 4%. A reduced 3% rate applies to land and houses acquired by March 31, 2027. Non-residential buildings remain at 4%.

Those rates alone do not tell me what I will owe. I would need the assessed value and the appropriate taxable base. For building land, the taxable base is halved for acquisitions by March 31, 2027.

Deductions and reductions can also exist for qualifying property. Their existence does not mean every investor qualifies. I would ask the relevant prefectural tax office or a qualified tax professional to check the property and acquisition details.

Why the purchase price is not enough

A purchase-price estimate can be a useful way to discuss a budget, but it is not the calculation base stated in the fact-check. Before relying on an estimate, I would ask where the assessed value came from and whether it separates the land from the building.

I would also ask how the building is classified for this tax. Calling a property an “investment” does not by itself answer whether the building is treated as a house or as a non-residential building for the stated rates.

Plan for a later notice

The prefecture sends the payment notice after the purchase. In practice, the notice commonly arrives about three to six months later, although timing varies by prefecture. That timing range is practitioner guidance, not a promise of when a particular notice will arrive.

For an overseas owner, I would clarify the contact and payment arrangements before closing. Ask the tax office and the professionals assisting you how notices will reach you, what information they need, and how you can pay from abroad. Do not assume that the absence of an acquisition-tax payment at closing means there is no tax.

Questions I would ask

  • What assessed values and classifications are being used for the land and building?
  • Does the reduced rate apply on the acquisition date?
  • Does the halved taxable base for building land apply?
  • Are any deductions or reductions available for this property?
  • Which prefecture will issue the notice, and where will it be sent?

A Japanese tax accountant's summary of the acquisition tax rules is a starting point for understanding the framework. A calculation for a particular purchase needs the property details and the applicable prefectural treatment.

For me, the practical lesson is to reserve for acquisition tax as a separate post-purchase item and obtain an estimate that explains the base, rate and any relief.
