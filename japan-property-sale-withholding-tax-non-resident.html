Japan Property Sale Withholding Tax for a Non-Resident Seller

When a non-resident sells Japanese land or buildings, the buyer generally withholds 10.21% of the sale price.
An exception applies if an individual buys it as a home for themselves or family for ¥100,000,000 or less.
The withheld amount is a prepayment; the seller settles the final tax in a Japanese return.

When I looked at the eventual sale of an investment property, I wanted to know whether the amount withheld at closing was the seller’s final tax. The fact-check says it is a prepayment. The seller’s final position is settled through a Japanese tax return.

That distinction affects cash planning. The withholding is calculated on the sale price under the stated rule, not presented in the fact-check as a calculation of the seller’s profit.

The buyer’s role

The general rule in the fact-check places the withholding obligation on the buyer when a non-resident sells Japanese land or buildings. The rate is 10.21% of the sale price, and the buyer pays the withheld amount to the tax office.

I would ask the professionals handling the sale to confirm the seller’s status, the buyer’s circumstances and the payment process before settlement. Both sides need to understand the amount that will be withheld and how the payment will be documented.

The discussion of the NTA rules provides a starting point. A tax professional can assess how they apply to the actual transaction.

The home-buyer exception

The stated exception has several conditions: the buyer is an individual, the property is bought as a home for the buyer or their family, and the price is ¥100,000,000 or less.

I would not assume the exception applies simply because the property is residential. I would ask the adviser to confirm the buyer’s status, intended use and price against the applicable rule.

The exception concerns withholding on this sale. It is not a general statement that a seller has no tax return or other tax obligations.

The return settles the final position

The amount withheld is a prepayment. The non-resident seller settles final Japanese tax in a return. I would ask a qualified tax professional to calculate the position using the actual sale and ownership records; the withholding amount alone does not establish the final liability.

I would also ask what proof of withholding and payment should be retained. The seller needs reliable records to support the return and any credit for tax withheld.

Questions I would resolve before settlement

  • Is the seller treated as a non-resident for this transaction?
  • Does the buyer have a withholding obligation?
  • Does the individual home-buyer exception apply on the actual facts?
  • How much will be withheld, and how will the payment be recorded?
  • What documents will the seller need for the Japanese return?
  • Who will assist the seller with the return and any required tax representation?

I would include the potential withholding in my sale cash-flow plan while keeping it separate from an estimate of final tax. That prevents the amount received at settlement from being mistaken for the entire economic or tax result of the sale.

The rule is an exit-planning question worth raising before a buyer and settlement arrangements are finalised. The right answer depends on the transaction and should be confirmed with the professionals responsible for it.
