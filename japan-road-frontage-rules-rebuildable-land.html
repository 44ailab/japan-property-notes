Road Frontage Rules in Japan: Land You Cannot Rebuild On

Some land in Japan generally cannot be built or rebuilt on because it does not meet road frontage requirements. Before paying a deposit, I would ask the licensed broker and municipality to verify the adjoining road’s legal status and check what can be built on the land.

What is the frontage requirement?

Under the Building Standards Act, a building lot must adjoin a legally recognised road by at least 2 metres. The road is generally required to be at least 4 metres wide. Land that fails the requirement generally cannot be built or rebuilt on.

The word “generally” matters here. I would not try to decide a particular lot’s status by looking at a photograph or measuring what appears to be a road. I would ask whether the adjoining road is legally recognised and whether the lot meets the applicable frontage requirement.

Non-rebuildable land exists and is sometimes advertised cheaply. A low advertised price would make me ask more questions about the land, including whether a future owner could rebuild. The answer needs to be checked for the particular lot.

A visible road is not the whole answer

When I look at a map or a listing, I may see a route beside a property. That tells me what appears to be there; it does not settle the route’s legal status.

I would ask the licensed broker to address the adjoining road in the Important Matters Explanation and check the legal status with the municipality before paying a deposit. I would also ask whether the lot satisfies the frontage requirement and what that means for building or rebuilding.

If the information is unclear, I would want the uncertainty explained. A description that simply says a property has road access would not answer all the questions I would bring to that check.

Other limits on what you can build

Road frontage is one part of the investigation. Land category, zoning, building coverage ratio and floor-area ratio also decide what can be built.

I would ask the licensed broker to explain the land category and zoning in the Important Matters Explanation, and verify them with the municipality before paying a deposit. I would then ask what the coverage and floor-area ratios mean for the building I have in mind.

A property can look suitable in a listing without answering those questions. I would describe my intended use clearly and ask the relevant professionals and municipality what needs to be verified for that specific land.

Check agricultural land separately

Agricultural land generally cannot be bought for building or converted without the Agricultural Committee’s permission. If the land category raises that possibility, I would ask about it before assuming that a building project can proceed.

I would want to know the confirmed land category and what permission question applies to the intended use. This is another reason to check the details before paying a deposit.

Questions to ask before you buy

  • Is the adjoining road legally recognised, and does the lot meet the applicable frontage requirement?
  • What do the Important Matters Explanation and municipality say about the land category and zoning?
  • What building coverage ratio and floor-area ratio apply, and what do they mean for my intended building?
  • If the land is agricultural, what should I ask the Agricultural Committee about permission?

FAQ

Can land beside a road still be non-rebuildable?

Yes. A lot must meet the applicable legal road and frontage requirements; the presence of a visible route alone does not settle its status. Ask the licensed broker and municipality to verify the particular lot.

Does a cheap listing mean the land cannot be rebuilt on?

Not necessarily. Non-rebuildable land is sometimes advertised cheaply, but price alone does not establish whether a lot can be rebuilt on. Check the road’s legal status and the lot’s circumstances.

Is road frontage the only building restriction to check?

No. Land category, zoning, building coverage ratio and floor-area ratio also affect what can be built. Agricultural land raises a separate permission question.
