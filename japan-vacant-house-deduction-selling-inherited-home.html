Selling an Inherited Vacant House in Japan: The Special Deduction

Last reviewed: November 21, 2026

Quick answer

A qualifying sale of an inherited vacant house may receive a special deduction of ¥30 million per heir, or ¥20 million per heir when three or more heirs inherit and the sale is on or after January 1, 2024. The house, inheritance, sale and paperwork must meet conditions. Living outside Japan does not by itself disqualify an heir.

Check the house and the person who lived there

I would start with the house itself. The stated rule covers an inherited house built on or before May 31, 1981. Condominiums are excluded.

The deceased person must have lived alone, with an exception for certain care-facility circumstances. That exception is a reason to check the details, not a reason to assume that every care-facility case qualifies.

The municipal confirmation certificate is required. Its Japanese name is 被相続人居住用家屋等確認書. I would identify the municipality and ask a tax professional how to obtain and use the certificate before relying on the deduction.

Check the sale deadline and price

The sale must take place by December 31 of the year three years after the inheritance began. The sale price must be no more than ¥100 million.

The program itself ends on December 31, 2027. Both the inheritance-based deadline and the program end date matter when planning a sale. I would check the dates together rather than working from only one of them.

These are eligibility conditions, not an estimate of the property’s sale price or the tax due.

Understand the deduction per heir

The stated deduction is ¥30 million per heir. When three or more heirs inherit, it is ¥20 million per heir for sales on or after January 1, 2024.

A deduction is not a cash payment. It is a figure used in the tax calculation when the conditions are met. I would ask for an individual calculation instead of assuming that the deduction will have the same effect for every heir.

For sales from 2024 through 2027, the buyer may carry out demolition or a seismic retrofit by February 15 of the year after the sale. The available fact does not mean that either step can be ignored; it identifies who may perform it and by when.

If you live outside Japan

The statutory wording applies to an individual who inherited, and residency alone does not disqualify a non-resident heir. A non-resident heir files through a tax agent, with the required municipal certificate.

There is also a separate inheritance tax filing question. The stated inheritance tax return and payment deadline is within 10 months from the day after learning of the death. For that topic, see the related page: Inheritance Tax in Japan for Foreign Heirs.

I would keep the inheritance tax deadline and the later sale conditions on separate lines in my notes. They concern different steps.

FAQ

Can a non-resident heir qualify?

Residency alone does not disqualify an heir. The other conditions and filing requirements still need checking.

Does an inherited condominium qualify?

No. Condominiums are excluded from this special deduction.

Is the municipal certificate optional?

No. The municipal confirmation certificate is required.
